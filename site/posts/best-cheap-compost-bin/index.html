Best Cheap Compost Bins (Indoor & Outdoor)

2026-08-26 · Garden & Outdoors

Composting is one of the easiest ways to cut down on kitchen waste and get free fertilizer for your garden, but the bins on the market can cost a fortune. You don't need to drop a hundred bucks on a stainless steel drum to get great results. I’ve tested and researched dozens of budget-friendly models, and I’m here to show you that a functional, durable bin is totally achievable for under $50.

What to Look For

Before you click "buy," here is what actually matters when you are shopping on a tight budget.

  • Size and Capacity: Match the bin to your household size and space. A 1-gallon countertop bin is fine for a single person, but a family of four will need a 5-gallon or larger option. For outdoor piles, look for at least 50 gallons to generate enough heat to break down material.
  • Odor Control: Look for tight-fitting lids and carbon filters. For indoor bins, a charcoal filter is a must-have to keep fruit flies out and smells contained. For outdoor bins, a secure lid is usually enough to keep pests out.
  • Material: Plastic is lightweight and cheap, but it can get brittle in cold weather. Metal is sturdier but rusts if not coated. For a low-cost option, thick, UV-stabilized polypropylene plastic is your best bet.
  • Ease of Access: Can you get the finished compost out easily? Bottom doors or removable side panels make harvesting a breeze. If you have to lift the entire bin to get the compost, you will hate it in six months.
  • Aeration: Compost needs oxygen. Look for bins with ventilation holes or a design that allows you to tumble or turn the pile. A solid, sealed bucket will turn into a stinky, anaerobic mess.
  • Assembly: Some tumblers require an afternoon with a wrench and a lot of swear words. Check reviews to see if the assembly is simple. I prefer bins that come in one piece or snap together without tools.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I empty my indoor compost bin?

For a 1.3 to 1.75-gallon countertop bin, you should empty it every two to three days to prevent odors and fruit flies. If you have a larger 2.4-gallon bin, you can stretch it to four or five days, especially if you keep the lid sealed and the filter fresh.

Can I compost indoors without a bin?

Yes, you can use a simple bowl or a large yogurt container, but you’ll need to empty it daily to avoid smells. A sealed bin with a carbon filter is really the cheapest way to go smell-free for a few days without spending much money.

What can I put in a cheap compost bin?

You can put fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, tea bags, eggshells, and shredded paper in any compost bin. Avoid meat, dairy, and oily foods in cheap indoor bins because they break down slowly and create bad odors that the basic filters can’t handle.
