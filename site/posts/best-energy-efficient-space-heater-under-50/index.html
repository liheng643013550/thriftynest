Winter is here, your living room feels like a walk-in fridge, and you are dreading the heating bill. You need a quick, targeted way to warm up your space without turning your thermostat into a money pit. The good news is that you don't need to spend a fortune on a fancy gadget to get the job done.

There are plenty of solid, energy-efficient space heaters out there for under $50. They won't heat your whole house, but they will keep you cozy in the room you're actually sitting in. Here are the best options for keeping warm without breaking the bank.

What to Look For

Before you click "buy," here is what actually matters when you are shopping on a budget.

If this helped, you'll also want to read Best Window Insulation Kits (Draft-Free Winters for Under $20) and How to Save on Laundry Costs (Energy, Water & Detergent).

  • Heating Technology: Look for ceramic or infrared models. Ceramic heaters are fast and efficient for small spaces. Infrared heaters warm objects and people directly, which feels great but can be slower to heat the air.
  • Safety Features: This is non-negotiable. You need an auto shut-off feature that triggers if the unit tips over or gets too hot. Look for a cool-touch exterior if you have pets or kids.
  • Thermostat and Settings: A heater with an adjustable thermostat is better than one with just "High" and "Low" settings. It allows the unit to cycle off when the room hits your desired temperature, saving electricity.
  • Size and Weight: You want something light enough to move from your office to your bedroom. A bulky unit that stays in the closet won't do you any good.
  • Wattage: Most 120-volt heaters max out around 1,500 watts. Higher wattage means more heat output, but it also means more electricity. For a small room, a 1,500-watt unit is the standard sweet spot.
  • Noise Level: If you are using this in a bedroom or while watching TV, check for "whisper quiet" or "noiseless" operation in the specs. Some fan-forced heaters are surprisingly loud.

Lasko 100 MyHeat

Price: Around $22

This little gadget is a huge seller for a reason. The Lasko MyHeat is a 200-watt personal heater that looks like a small wall panel. It is incredibly cheap and incredibly simple. You plug it directly into a wall outlet, flip a switch, and you get instant heat.

What makes it great is the cost to run. Because it is only 200 watts, it uses a fraction of the electricity of a standard 1,500-watt heater. It is perfect for warming just your feet under your desk or the immediate area around your chair. It is also barely larger than a smartphone, making it perfect for small apartments where space is at a premium.

The downsides are obvious: it only warms the area directly in front of it. If you are sitting three feet away, you won't feel the heat. It also lacks a thermostat, so it just runs until you turn it off.

Who should buy it: If you work from home and have cold feet, or if you just need a tiny, cheap boost of warmth in one specific spot, this is the one. It is the ultimate "personal" heater for a tight budget.

Amazon Basics Ceramic Space Heater

Price: Around $30

You can't beat the value here. The Amazon Basics Ceramic Space Heater is a no-frills, boxy heater that does exactly what it says. It features a fan-forced ceramic heating element that pushes warm air into the room. It has two heat settings (750W and 1500W) and a cool-air fan option for the summer.

What makes it great is the simplicity and the safety record. It feels sturdy for the price, and it has the standard tip-over and overheat protection. It is small enough to sit on a side table but powerful enough to heat a small office or bedroom effectively. The fan is a bit noisy, but it is a "white noise" hum rather than a rattling sound.

The downsides are cosmetic. It is not the prettiest device, and the plastic casing can get warm to the touch, though not dangerously hot. There is no remote and no digital display, just simple dials.

Who should buy it: If you want a reliable workhorse for a small room and don't care about bells and whistles, this is your best bet. It is the definition of "just works."

Honeywell HCE200W UberHeat

Price: Around $35

Honeywell is a trusted name in home comfort, and the UberHeat is a classic. This is a compact ceramic heater that is designed to be moved around easily. It has a built-in carrying handle on the back, which is more useful than you might think.

What makes it great is its focused heat delivery. It uses a high-performance ceramic plate and a fan to blow heat in one direction. It warms up in seconds and is very effective at heating a person sitting nearby quickly. The exterior stays cool to the touch, which is a huge plus if you have curious toddlers or clumsy pets. It also has two heat settings and an adjustable thermostat, so you can dial in the exact temperature you want.

The downside is that it is a spot heater. It won't circulate warm air around a large, open-plan living area very well. The fan is also audible, similar to a small desk fan on high.

Who should buy it: Anyone looking for a safe, portable heater to keep next to the couch or in the bedroom. The cool-touch housing makes it one of the safest choices in this price range.

GiveBest Portable Electric Heater

Price: Around $40

This is one of the most popular budget heaters on the market, and it is easy to see why. The GiveBest heater is a compact, tower-style unit that punches well above its weight. It features a digital display and a remote control, which is rare at this price point.

What makes it great is the combination of power and control. It has a PTC ceramic heating element that produces a lot of heat very quickly. The digital thermostat is more accurate than the dials on cheaper models, so it can maintain a steady room temperature without constantly cycling on and off. The remote control lets you adjust the temperature or turn it off from the comfort of your bed or couch. It is also very quiet in operation, making it great for a bedroom.

The downsides? The build quality is decent, but it feels a little plasticky. The digital display is bright, which might bother you if you are trying to sleep in a dark room.

Who should buy it: If you want a "smart" feel without the smart price tag. The remote and digital thermostat make it the most convenient option on this list.

Lasko 754200 Ceramic Heater

Price: Around $45

If you need to heat a larger area, this Lasko model is a step up. It is a taller, oscillating tower heater that looks like a sleek appliance rather than a box. The oscillation feature is the main selling point here.

What makes it great is the even heat distribution. Instead of blasting one spot, the head rotates back and forth, pushing warm air across a wider area. This makes it much better for a living room or a large master bedroom. It has an adjustable thermostat, an electronic timer, and a remote control. It also has a digital display that shows the current temperature. It runs fairly quietly for a fan-forced heater, with a low hum that fades into the background.

The downside is that it is bulkier than the other options. It takes up more floor space, and the plastic body can feel a bit flimsy if you bump into it.

Who should buy it: If you want to heat a shared living space or a larger bedroom, the oscillation makes this the best choice. It gets the heat out of the corner and into the middle of the room.

Dreo Space Heater

Price: Around $49

The Dreo is the most feature-packed heater on this list. It is slightly over the $50 mark sometimes, but you can often catch it on sale for under that. It is a premium-feeling tower heater that focuses on precise temperature control and safety.

What makes it great is its advanced thermostat. It can maintain the room temperature within a single degree of your target, which is impressive. It has an eco mode that automatically adjusts the power output to save energy, which is exactly what you want for your electric bill. It is also exceptionally quiet, making it ideal for nurseries or light sleepers. The LED display is dimmable, and the remote control is intuitive.

The downside is that it doesn't oscillate like the Lasko, so it has a narrower heat throw. It is also the most expensive option here, so if you are strictly on a $40 budget, this might be a stretch.

Who should buy it: If you want the most energy-efficient and quietest operation possible, the Dreo is the winner. It is perfect for a bedroom where you want the temperature to stay perfectly stable all night.

The Bottom Line

You do not need to spend hundreds of dollars to stay warm. For under $50, you can find a heater that will efficiently warm your immediate space and keep your utility bill manageable.

For a tiny budget and a specific spot, get the Lasko MyHeat. For the best all-around value in a small room, grab the Amazon Basics or the Honeywell. If you need to heat a bigger living area, the oscillating Lasko 754200 is your best friend. And if you want the quietest, most precise heating for a bedroom, the Dreo is worth the extra few dollars.

Just remember: size the heater to the room. A $20 heater will not heat a 400-square-foot basement. Use these as zone heaters to supplement your central heating, and you will stay comfortable all season long.
