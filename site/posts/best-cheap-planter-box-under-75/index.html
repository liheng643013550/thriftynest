If you have a small balcony, a narrow side yard, or just one sunny windowsill, the whole planter box question comes down to a single decision: how much floor space can you actually give up? Everything else — material, drainage, price — follows from that. Get the footprint wrong and the best-built box in the world becomes a trip hazard you regret by August.

This guide is for the home cook squeezing herbs and a few vegetables out of a tight space on a tight budget. Every pick below stays under about $75 and is chosen first for how little room it takes up, then for what it grows.

What actually matters

  • Footprint, not volume. A 37-gallon tub sounds generous until you realize it needs roughly two feet by two feet of floor. Measure your space before you fall for a big capacity number.
  • Depth for what you grow. Herbs and lettuce are happy in 6 to 8 inches of soil. Tomatoes and peppers usually want 10 to 12 inches or more. A shallow box that fits your railing may not fit a tomato.
  • Drainage. Roots rot in a box that holds water. Look for pre-drilled holes or plan to drill your own. A saucer or tray keeps the mess off your floor.
  • Material and weight. Resin and fabric are light and cheap; wood looks better but costs more and needs care. A filled box is heavy — a 37-gallon planter can weigh well over 100 pounds once it is full of wet soil.
  • Soil is part of the budget. A cheap box plus a bag of decent mix is the real cost. Budget roughly $10 to $20 for potting mix on top of the container.
  • Vertical vs. floor space. A tall, narrow box or a stackable pot uses inches of floor and feet of height. On a balcony, that trade is usually worth it.

Outsunny Walk-In Greenhouse

Rough price band: usually around $60 to $75 for the smaller sizes.

Not a planter box in the traditional sense, but worth naming for a specific small-space problem: you want to grow more than the floor allows. A walk-in greenhouse lets you stack shelves and grow vertically, so a narrow strip of yard does the work of a much bigger footprint. Owner reviews often mention it as a budget way to extend the season for herbs and starts.

Be honest about the downsides. It is not a permanent structure, and the cover typically lasts a few seasons before it needs replacing.

Who should buy it: a gardener with a small but real patch of ground who wants vertical growing room more than a single big box.

Miracle-Gro Potting Mix

Rough price band: usually around $10 to $20 for a standard bag, varying by size and seller.

Not a box, but it belongs in this guide because soil is the hidden line item. A budget planter box is only as good as what you fill it with, and a decent potting mix with drainage and slow-release feeding saves you from buying fertilizer separately. Owner reviews consistently report it as a reliable, no-surprise choice for containers.

Downsides are minor but real: it is heavier and messier than some premium mixes, and a big bag goes fast if you fill a 37-gallon box. Factor the bag into your total before you commit to a large container.

Who should buy it: anyone buying any box on this list — budget for it up front.

Fiskars Transplanter

Rough price band: usually around $10 to $15.

The tool that makes a small box work harder. A transplanter lets you tuck seedlings into tight spots and lift weeds without disturbing neighboring roots — exactly what you need when every inch of soil counts. Owner reviews often mention the sturdy build and comfortable handle for the price.

Downsides are the usual for a hand tool: it is not for heavy digging, and the pointed blade can bend if you pry with it. It is a supplement, not a replacement for a full trowel.

Who should buy it: anyone planting densely in a compact box and wanting a cheap tool that does one job well.

The bottom line

Decide your footprint first, then buy. If you have a patio or a wide balcony, the FCMP Outdoor 37 Gallon gives you the most growing room for the money. If you have a windowsill or a narrow railing, the OXO Good Grips 1.75 Gallon is the honest choice — small, movable, and enough for fresh herbs. Add a bag of Miracle-Gro Potting Mix to either, and a cheap hand tool or watering can if your budget allows. Keep the total under $75 and you will still have room for seeds.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much floor space does the FCMP Outdoor 37 Gallon take up?

It is a large container, so plan on roughly two feet by two feet of floor, and note that it gets very heavy once filled with wet soil. Owner reviews often mention it is best placed where you intend to keep it.

Can I grow tomatoes in the OXO Good Grips 1.75 Gallon?

No — at 1.75 gallons it is suited to herbs and lettuce, and reviewers often mention that bigger plants outgrow it quickly. For tomatoes you want a much larger box like the FCMP Outdoor 37 Gallon.

Do I need to buy soil separately from the planter box?

Yes. The boxes on this list do not come filled, so budget for a bag of Miracle-Gro Potting Mix, usually around $10 to $20, on top of the container price.
