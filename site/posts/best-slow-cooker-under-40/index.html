Best Slow Cookers Under $40 in 2026: Set It and Forget It, Cheaply

2026-08-18 · Kitchen & Cooking

A slow cooker is one of the few kitchen appliances that genuinely pays for itself. You can turn a $3 chuck roast into a meal that feeds a family of four for two days. But you don’t need to spend $100 on a smart model with a phone app to get tender, set-it-and-forget-it results. In fact, the best slow cookers under $40 do the exact same job as the premium ones: they cook low and slow for eight hours without burning your dinner.

The trick is knowing which budget models have reliable temperature controls and which ones run too hot. Here are the best cheap slow cookers in 2026 that won’t let you down.

What to Look For in a Budget Slow Cooker

Before you hit buy, run through these five criteria. They matter more than the brand name.

  • Stoneware insert vs. metal: You want a removable stoneware (ceramic) pot. It heats evenly and cleans up easily. Avoid old-school metal inserts that scorch food and are impossible to scrub.
  • High and Low settings: This sounds obvious, but some mini cookers only have one heat setting. Make sure you can control the temperature. A "Warm" setting is a bonus, not a requirement.
  • Size vs. your household: A 6-quart model is the sweet spot for families and meal prepping. A 4-quart is fine for couples. A 2-quart is only good for dips and small sides. Buy the size that matches how you actually cook.
  • Manual controls: Skip digital timers on budget models. They break, and the buttons are finicky. A simple dial with Off, Low, High, and Warm is more reliable and easier to clean.
  • Dishwasher-safe parts: The whole point of a slow cooker is convenience. If the stoneware and lid aren't dishwasher-safe, you are going to hate washing it by hand every time. Check the fine print.

Hamilton Beach 33061 6-Quart

Price: ~$35

This is the no-brainer pick. The Hamilton Beach 33061 is the manual slow cooker that most budget roundups agree on. It has a simple dial with Off, Low, High, and Warm. No digital screen, no timers, nothing to break. The 6-quart stoneware insert is hefty and heavy, which actually helps with even heat distribution.

The downsides? The "Warm" setting runs a little hot on some units, so if you leave it on Warm for four hours, your food might dry out. Also, the cord is short. You'll need an outlet close to your counter.

Who should buy it: Anyone who wants a dependable workhorse for Sunday pot roasts, chili, and shredded chicken. If you want zero fuss and a big capacity, this is the one.

Price: ~$25

Who should buy it: Singles, couples, or empty nesters who want to make a small batch of soup or a few servings of pulled pork without leftovers. It is also a great first slow cooker for a college student.

Proctor Silex 33080 5-Quart

Price: ~$20

If you want to spend as little as possible, this is the best option. The Proctor Silex 33080 is a bare-bones 5-quart slow cooker. It has a simple knob, a glass lid, and a stoneware insert. It cooks food exactly like a $100 model.

There are real compromises at this price point. The "Low" setting is actually pretty hot, closer to what other brands call "Medium." You will need to check your food an hour earlier than your recipe calls for. The plastic handles on the side get hot to the touch, so use a towel when moving it.

Who should buy it: Anyone on a strict budget or someone who is not sure if they will actually use a slow cooker. At $20, it is a low-risk way to test the waters.

Hamilton Beach 33226 6-Quart Programmable

Price: ~$35

This is a bit of a cheat because it is technically a programmable model, but it often dips below the $40 mark. The Hamilton Beach 33226 has a digital timer, a probe thermometer for meat, and a "Keep Warm" cycle. It is the most feature-rich option on this list.

The advantages are real. You can set it to cook for 8 hours and walk away, and it will automatically switch to warm. The probe thermometer is excellent for pork shoulder or a roast, ensuring you get the exact internal temperature you want.

Who should buy it: Busy families who want to program a meal in the morning and come home to perfectly cooked food that is automatically kept warm. This is the best value if you want "smart" features without the high-end price.

The Bottom Line

You do not need to spend a fortune to get a great slow cooker. The Hamilton Beach 33061 is the best overall pick because it is cheap, reliable, and big enough for a family. If you want the absolute lowest price and are willing to babysit the temperature a little, the Proctor Silex 33080 is a steal. And if you want a hands-off experience with a timer and meat probe, the Hamilton Beach 33226 is the smartest choice for just a few dollars more.

Skip the fancy internet-connected models. One of these budget slow cookers will make the same tender, flavorful dinner for a fraction of the cost. Set it, forget it, and save your money for the groceries.
