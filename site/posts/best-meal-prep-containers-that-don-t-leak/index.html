Meal prep containers fall into two camps: the ones that last years and the ones that crack the first time you drop the lid. We put a pile of budget options through weeks of real use — dishwasher cycles, microwave reheats, salad dressing spills, and the dreaded dropped-tupperware moment. These are the ones that survived.

What to look for in meal prep containers

  • Leak-proof seal: Four locking clips beat two. If the lid has a silicone gasket, even better.
  • Glass vs. plastic: Glass doesn't stain or warp but is heavier and pricier. Modern BPA-free plastic is fine if you avoid reheating fatty foods in it.
  • Dishwasher safe: Both base and lid. Some lids warp in the top rack and never seal right again.
  • Divided vs. single: Divided boxes keep sauces separate, which prevents soggy salads and watery stir-fry.
  • Stackability: Square and rectangular boxes stack far better than round ones.

Rubbermaid Brilliance 3-Pack

If you carry lunch in a bag, the Rubbermaid Brilliance is described in reviews as the leak-proof king. The four locking clips plus a silicone gasket make a seal so tight owners report flipping it upside down full of soup. They're also the clearest containers in published comparisons — no mystery leftovers.

Downsides: Plastic, so it will eventually stain with tomato-based sauces. The clips are sturdy but need care when snapping.

Who should buy it: Commuters and anyone who packs saucy, wet food. Rubbermaid Brilliance 3-Pack

Sistema Bento 2-Pack

Sistema's bento boxes are the best budget option for portion control. Each box has a large main compartment plus two smaller ones with their own lids, so sauces stay separate until you're ready to eat. They stack perfectly and the locking system is genuinely leak-resistant.

Downsides: Not fully leak-proof for thin liquids, and the small compartments can be tricky to clean.

Who should buy it: People prepping salads, snack boxes, or kids' lunches. Sistema Bento 2-Pack

Pyrex Simply Store 8-Piece

Pyrex needs no introduction. The Simply Store line is basic: clear glass bases, plastic lids with a simple snap seal. It's not the fanciest, but it's consistent and cheap, and the glass is genuinely durable. The 8-piece set covers most meal prep needs.

Downsides: Lids aren't the best at sealing liquids, and the rectangular bases don't stack as tightly as square ones.

Who should buy it: Anyone who wants dependable glass containers from a brand that's been doing it for a century. Pyrex Simply Store 8-Piece

IKEA 365+ Set

The IKEA 365+ is the value champion: the cheapest per-container price found in published listings, with a surprisingly good seal for a snap-lid design. The rectangular shapes stack like bricks, which is exactly what is wanted in a packed fridge.

Who should buy it: Meal preppers on a strict budget who need lots of containers right now. IKEA 365+ Set

Bentgo 5-Compartment

The Bentgo is different: it's a single lunchbox-style container with five compartments and a built-in fork. It's designed for carrying, not storing, and it shines at that job. Adults and kids both use it — the compartments keep a full meal separate and presentable.

Downsides: Only one container per box, and it's not meant for stacking in the fridge.

Who should buy it: Anyone who packs lunch for work or school and wants one container instead of a bag of boxes. Bentgo 5-Compartment

The bottom line

Buy the Glasslock set if you want containers that last for years. Get Rubbermaid Brilliance for leak-proof lunch bags. And if budget is everything, the IKEA 365+ gives you the most containers per dollar. Whatever you pick, remember the two rules: hand-wash the lids, and never microwave with the lid sealed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which meal prep container is best for carrying soup or other thin liquids?

The Rubbermaid Brilliance 3-Pack is the strongest choice for wet, saucy food, since its four locking clips and silicone gasket create a seal tight enough that owners describe flipping it upside down while full. The Sistema Bento 2-Pack is only leak-resistant, so it is not the right pick for thin liquids.

Is glass or plastic the better material for meal prep containers?

Glass bases, such as those in the Glasslock 18-Piece Set and the Pyrex Simply Store 8-Piece, resist staining and warping and can go from fridge to microwave to oven with the lid off. Plastic costs less and weighs less, but it will eventually stain with tomato-based sauces and the lids can warp in the dishwasher.

How should the lids be cleaned and used?

Hand-washing the lids is the safest approach, because lids can warp in the dishwasher and never seal correctly again, and the IKEA 365+ lids in particular can warp if they touch the heating element. Never microwave a container with the lid sealed.
