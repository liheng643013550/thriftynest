The one question that decides everything with cheap window insulation kits is this: does the kit cut noise as well as cold, or does it just stop the draft? For a couple in a first apartment, that matters more than the price tag. You want a warmer room at night without turning your bedroom into a sealed box that keeps every footstep from the hallway echoing inside.

What actually matters

  • Noise, not just cold. Most shrink-film kits are marketed as draft stoppers. Some also dampen outside noise noticeably; others do almost nothing for sound and can even make a room feel more enclosed. If street noise or a loud neighbor keeps you up, prioritize kits that reviewers specifically mention as quieting the room.
  • Whether it disturbs neighbors. Shrink film goes on with double-sided tape and a hair dryer. That heat gun or dryer runs for a few minutes, and the tape pulls off at move-out. No drilling, no hammering, so nothing travels through the wall into the apartment next door.
  • Fit and window type. Kits are sized by window dimensions and by whether you have a single- or double-hung window, a slider, or a patio door. Measure the glass plus the frame before buying.
  • Installation effort and reversibility. Film kits are renter-friendly because they peel off. Rigid panels and magnetic kits cost more and take more space to store, but they come down and go back up season after season.
  • Cost per window and per year. A basic film kit usually runs a few dollars per window. That is a small number next to the heating bill, and it is the main reason this category exists.
  • Condensation risk. Sealing a window tight can trap moisture against cold glass, especially in a bathroom or kitchen. If your apartment already has foggy windows, ventilation matters as much as insulation.

Rough price band: typically under $15 for a multi-window pack.

This is the best-known name in the category, and the reason is simple: it is a clear shrink film plus double-sided tape, and it works on almost any window shape. The specs show it covers standard windows, and you trim the excess with scissors. Owner reviews consistently report that a hair dryer tightens the film into a nearly invisible sheet.

On noise, the honest answer is mixed. A single layer of stretched film blocks some high-frequency sound, so traffic hiss and wind can soften. It will not stop a bass-heavy stereo or a loud conversation through a shared wall. Several reviewers mention the room feeling slightly quieter but not soundproof.

Downsides: you cannot open the window without cutting the film, and the tape can lift paint if you remove it carelessly. Buy it if you want the cheapest, most forgiving first attempt and you mainly care about drafts.

Window Insulation Kit by Frost King

Rough price band: usually $10 to $20 depending on window count.

Frost King sells essentially the same idea as 3M, sometimes with more tape or a larger film sheet per box. The trade-off is that film thickness and tape quality vary by the specific kit, so read the box carefully rather than assuming every Frost King kit is identical.

Where it earns its place is value. If you have four or five windows in a first apartment, a multi-window kit brings the per-window cost down to a couple of dollars. Reviewers often mention that the tape holds through a full winter.

On noise, expect the same modest effect as any film kit. It helps with wind and light traffic noise. It does not meaningfully block low frequencies.

Downsides: the film can be fussy to get smooth on very large windows, and a bad tape seal means the film peels at a corner by mid-January. Buy it if you have several windows and want the lowest cost per window.

Amazon Basics Ceramic Space Heater

Rough price band: typically $30 to $45.

This is not an insulation kit, and that is the point. If your windows are already as sealed as they are going to get and the room is still cold, a small ceramic heater warms the space you are actually sitting in rather than fighting the whole apartment.

The spec that matters for a shared building is wattage. Small ceramic heaters like this usually run around 750W on low and 1,500W on high, which is close to the limit of a single 15-amp circuit. Running one on high plus a microwave on the same breaker is a recipe for a tripped circuit.

On noise, this is where it can bother a partner or a neighbor. Ceramic heaters have a fan, and cheap fans whine. Owner reviews are split: some describe a gentle hum, others say the fan is audible across a quiet room. If your partner is a light sleeper, that matters more than the heat output.

Downsides: it heats a small area, not a whole apartment, and it raises the electric bill. Buy it if you want spot warmth at your desk or beside the couch.

Lasko 754200 Ceramic Heater

Rough price band: usually $25 to $40.

Lasko has sold this basic ceramic heater for years, and it shows in the review volume. Specs put it in the same 1,500W class as the Amazon Basics unit, with a manual dial and two heat settings. It is small enough to sit on a nightstand or under a desk.

The noise story is the same as any fan-forced heater: there is a fan, and you will hear it. Reviewers frequently describe it as white-noise-ish rather than silent. That is fine for daytime, less fine if one of you needs dead quiet to fall asleep.

Downsides: no thermostat precision, and the plastic housing gets warm to the touch. Buy it if you want a cheap, proven spot heater and you are not chasing silence.

Dreo Space Heater

Rough price band: typically $40 to $70.

Dreo's small space heaters sit a step up in build and control. Specs usually list multiple heat levels, a timer, and a quieter fan design than the bargain-bin heaters. For a couple sharing a bedroom, the timer is the useful part: it runs for a set period and shuts off rather than cycling all night.

On noise, owner reviews are more favorable here than on the cheapest heaters, though "quiet" is relative. Most describe a low hum you can sleep through. A few still find it noticeable in a silent room.

Downsides: it costs more than the basic ceramic units, and it is still a 1,500W-class appliance, so it is not a whole-room solution. Buy it if you want a heater you can run on a timer without it sounding like a small jet.

Pro Breeze Electric Mini Dehumidifier

Rough price band: usually $40 to $60.

Here is the connection people miss. A sealed window keeps heat in and moisture in. In a small apartment, that moisture shows up as condensation on cold glass, and condensation is exactly how window frames rot and how mold gets a foothold. A mini dehumidifier pulls some of that water out of the air.

Specs for units this size typically list a small tank, often around 16 ounces or so, which means frequent emptying in a damp room. It is a maintenance item, not a set-and-forget appliance.

On noise, these are quiet but not silent. Reviewers often compare the sound to a small fan. It is unlikely to bother a neighbor through a wall.

Downsides: small capacity, frequent emptying, and it does not heat the room. Buy it if your windows fog up after you seal them.

Amazon Smart Plug

Rough price band: typically $15 to $25.

This is the cheap fix for the sleep problem. If your heater has a mechanical switch you can leave in the on position, a smart plug lets you schedule it to run for an hour before bed and shut off at a set time. That means no fan noise at 3 a.m. and no arguing about who gets up to turn it off.

The noise it makes is a single relay click when it switches, which is quieter than any heater fan.

Downsides: it needs a 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network, and it does not work with heaters that require a physical button press to restart. Buy it if your main complaint is a heater running all night.

The bottom line

For a couple in a first apartment, start with film. The 3M Indoor Shrink Film Kit or the Window Insulation Kit by Frost King costs a few dollars per window, installs with a hair dryer in minutes, and comes off without a drill or a landlord conversation. Neither one will make a noisy street disappear, and anyone who promises that is overselling.

If cold is still the problem after sealing, add spot heat rather than trying to warm the whole apartment. The Dreo Space Heater is the better pick for a shared bedroom because of the timer and the quieter fan; the Lasko 754200 Ceramic Heater is the cheaper, louder option. Pair either with an Amazon Smart Plug so it shuts off on schedule instead of humming all night.

And if your windows fog up once they are sealed, deal with the moisture before it becomes a mold problem. That is the one step most renters skip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do window insulation kits actually reduce noise?

Somewhat, and mostly the higher frequencies. A single layer of stretched film can soften wind and light traffic noise, but reviewers consistently say it does not block bass or loud conversation through a shared wall. If noise is your main problem, treat film as a small help, not a solution.

Will installing a kit disturb my neighbors?

No. Film kits use double-sided tape and a hair dryer, so there is no drilling or hammering and nothing travels through the wall. The main sound is a few minutes of hair dryer noise, which is quieter than a vacuum.

Is a space heater or an insulation kit better for a cold apartment?

They solve different problems. A film kit like the 3M Indoor Shrink Film Kit or the Window Insulation Kit by Frost King reduces drafts for a few dollars per window. A 1,500W-class heater such as the Dreo Space Heater or the Lasko 754200 Ceramic Heater warms the spot you are sitting in, but it adds to the electric bill and its fan can be audible at night.
