How Much Do Microfiber Cloths Cost? Real Numbers, Not Guesses

Microfiber cloths are one of those purchases where the sticker price tells you almost nothing. A pack can cost less than a sandwich or more than a decent dinner, and the difference rarely shows up on the tag. If you're renting your first small place and trying to figure out what a year of cleaning actually costs, here are the real numbers, based on published specs, manufacturer listings, and what verified owners consistently report.

The quick answer

Most households pay between $8 and $30 for the first year of microfiber cloths. That assumes you buy one multi-pack, use the cloths for everything from counters to mirrors, and replace a few of them as they wear out or get lost.

If you buy a budget bulk pack and wash them properly, you can land closer to $8–$12 for the year. If you buy premium cloths, use them for a specific job like glass or stainless, and add a couple of specialty items, you'll land closer to $25–$30. The cloths themselves are cheap. The cost that surprises people is the one nobody warns them about: buying the wrong size pack, then buying again.

How the number is built

There's no wattage here, so the formula is simpler than a space heater or a vacuum. For cloths, the first-year cost works out to:

(price of the pack ÷ number of cloths in the pack) × how many you actually use + replacements + any accessories

Three things move that number:

  • Pack size. A 24-pack at a low per-cloth price beats a 4-pack at a high one, but only if you'll use 24. In a studio or one-bedroom, most renters genuinely use 8–12 cloths on rotation and keep the rest as spares.
  • Replacement rate. Cloths don't last forever. Owner reviews often mention that thin, cheap cloths start shedding lint or losing their grab after roughly 20–40 washes, while heavier cloths hold up longer. Budget for replacing a few per year, not the whole pack.
  • Accessories. A squeegee or a spray mop isn't a cloth, but it changes how many cloths you burn through. If you're mopping a small kitchen floor with a dedicated mop, you're not using six cloths to do it.

For a small apartment, a realistic first-year math looks like this: one 12-pack at roughly $10–$15, plus two or three replacement cloths at around $1–$2 each, plus maybe one accessory. That's the $8–$30 range.

What pushes the cost up

Buying small packs of premium cloths. A four-pack of branded cloths can run $10–$15, which is $2.50–$3.75 per cloth. The same money spent on a 12-pack gets you three times the cloths. Premium cloths are genuinely better for some jobs — glass, stainless, car interiors — but for wiping a kitchen counter, the difference is hard to justify.

Buying several "specialized" sets. Glass cloths, dusting cloths, polishing cloths, scrub cloths. Each set is $8–$15, and suddenly you've spent $40 on cloths for a 500-square-foot apartment. Most renters do fine with one general-purpose pack plus one glass-friendly cloth.

Letting them walk away. Cloths get used as potholders, wiped across paint, left in a bucket, or tossed because they smell. Owner reviews frequently mention odor as the number one reason cloths get replaced early. Rinsing them promptly and washing them without fabric softener (softener clogs the fibers) is the cheapest maintenance habit you can build.

Adding a mop system you didn't need. If you buy a spray mop for a small hard-floor area, you've added $25–$40 to a job a $2 cloth could partly handle. That's not wasted money if you mop often, but it's not a cloth cost.

What pushes the cost down

Buy in bulk once. The Amazon Basics Microfiber Cloths is the reference point most renters start with — a large multi-pack at a low per-cloth price. Per-cloth cost in bulk packs like this typically lands under a dollar, sometimes well under.

Try a mid-size branded pack if you want thicker cloths. The HOMEXCEL 12-Pack and the Mr. Siga 12-Pack sit in the middle of the market — more cloths than a premium four-pack, and reviews often describe them as holding up reasonably through repeated washing. The Zwipes 24-Pack is the bulk option if you want a big pile and don't mind a thinner cloth.

Look at E-Cloth if you want fewer, better cloths. The E-Cloth General Purpose Pack is a smaller pack at a higher per-cloth price. Owner reviews often mention that these cloths work well with just water, which can reduce how much you spend on sprays. For a small space, fewer cloths that do more can beat a big cheap pile.

Consider Scotch-Brite if you want a familiar brand. The Scotch-Brite Large Microfiber Cloths are a widely available option, usually priced in the middle of the range. The Scrub Daddy Microfiber Cloths are another branded choice, typically at the higher end per cloth.

Add one accessory that saves cloths. A Squeegee for shower glass means you're not drying the whole door with a cloth every time. A plastic scraper handles stuck food so you're not scrubbing a cloth to death. Neither is required, and both add to the first-year total — but they can stretch your cloths further.

If you mop, pick one system and stop. The O-Cedar EasyWring Spin Mop and the Vileda Turbo Microfiber Spin Mop are the two names renters ask about most. The Rubbermaid Reveal Spray Mop and the Bona Hardwood Floor Spray Mop are spray-style options for hard floors. All of these use microfiber pads you wash and reuse. Buying one is fine. Buying two is how a $12 cloth budget becomes a $60 floor-care budget.

Typical first-year scenarios

Scenario What you buy Typical first-year cost

Standard one-bedroom One mid-range 12-pack plus a few replacements $12–$20
Premium-leaning Smaller premium pack plus one glass cloth $20–$30
Cloths plus one floor tool Mid-range pack plus a spin or spray mop $40–$65

The jump from row one to row five is almost entirely accessories, not cloths. Keep that in mind before you add anything to the cart.

The bottom line

Microfiber cloths are one of the cheapest cleaning purchases you'll make. For a first apartment, plan on $8–$20 for the cloths themselves in year one, and treat anything above that as an accessory decision rather than a cloth decision. The cloths are not where the money goes. The extras are.

Three concrete ways to pay less:

  1. Buy one bulk or mid-size pack and stop shopping. A single 12- or 24-pack covers a small apartment for a year. Resist the second "just for glass" purchase unless you genuinely need it.
  2. Wash them right. No fabric softener, no dryer sheets, and don't let them sit damp in a bucket. Owner reviews repeatedly point to odor and fiber breakdown as the reason cloths get replaced early.
  3. Buy one floor tool, not three. If you mop, pick a spin mop or a spray mop and commit. If you don't mop much, skip it entirely and use a cloth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I expect to spend on microfiber cloths in my first year of renting?

Most renters spend between $8 and $30 for the first year, depending on pack size and whether you add accessories. A budget bulk pack alone can land near $8–$12, while a premium-leaning setup with a glass cloth runs closer to $20–$30.

Is it cheaper to buy a 24-pack or a 12-pack of microfiber cloths?

Per cloth, a 24-pack like the Zwipes 24-Pack usually costs less than a 12-pack, but only if you'll actually use that many. In a small apartment, most people rotate 8–12 cloths and keep the rest as spares, so a 12-pack is often the better fit.

Do microfiber cloths need to be replaced every year?

No. Cloths typically last through many washes, though thin budget cloths may start shedding or losing grab after roughly 20–40 washes according to owner reviews. Budget for replacing a few per year rather than the whole pack.
