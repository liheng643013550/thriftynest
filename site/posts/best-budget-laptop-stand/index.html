What to look for

  • Height and adjustability. A fixed stand lifts your screen 2-4 inches, which helps but may not get the top of the screen to eye level. Adjustable stands let you fine-tune between sitting and standing setups.
  • Weight capacity and stability. Cheap stands wobble when you type on the laptop keyboard. Look for a stated weight limit (most handle 10-22 lbs) and rubber pads on the base.
  • Material. Aluminum and steel run cooler and last longer. ABS plastic is lighter and cheaper but flexes under a 5-lb gaming laptop.
  • Ventilation. Open-frame or slotted designs let heat escape. A solid slab of plastic can turn your laptop's underside into a griddle.
  • Portability. If you move between a desk, a couch, and a coffee shop, a folding stand that fits in a laptop sleeve beats a heavy riser every time.
  • Cable and accessory clearance. Some low stands leave no room for a USB hub or external drive underneath. Check the gap before you buy.

Pair any of these with a $15 wireless keyboard and mouse. Raising the screen without dropping your hands to desk level just trades neck pain for wrist pain, in my experience.
