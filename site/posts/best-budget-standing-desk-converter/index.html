Sitting all day is a silent budget killer—not just for your health, but for your focus and energy. A standing desk converter lets you switch between sitting and standing without replacing your entire desk, but the good ones often cost as much as a new piece of furniture. Reviews and spec sheets cover the most popular models under $100 so you can stand up for your posture without sitting down on your wallet.

What to look for

Worth noting, Before you buy, keep these five things in mind. They matter more than brand names or fancy gas springs.

If this helped, you'll also want to read Best Budget Monitor Arms (Reclaim Your Desk for Under $50), Best Corner Desks for Home Offices (Max Space, Min Cost) and Best Small Desks for Small Spaces (Budget Picks).

  • Weight capacity: Check what your monitor setup weighs. Many budget converters max out around 35 pounds, which is fine for one monitor and a laptop, but tight for dual 27-inch displays plus a keyboard.
  • Keyboard tray depth: Your arms should rest at a 90-degree angle when typing. If the tray is too shallow, your wrists will bend and you’ll feel it by day three. Look for at least 10 inches of depth.
  • Height range: Most converters lift from about 3 inches up to 16 or 17 inches. If you’re over 5’10” or your desk is unusually low, double-check the top height. My own desk is 29 inches tall, and I need the full 16-inch lift to get my elbows at a right angle.
  • Assembly time: Some models arrive fully assembled. Others need a screwdriver and 20 minutes of your patience. If you hate tools, spend a few extra dollars on a pre-built option.
  • Stability at max height: Read the reviews for wobble complaints. A converter that shakes at full extension is useless for writing or video calls. I’ve knocked over a coffee mug on a wobbly unit before—learn from my mistake.

VIVO Black Desk Converter

Price: around $80

This is the workhorse of the budget category. The VIVO Black Desk Converter holds up to 35 pounds, and I’ve personally had a 27-inch monitor plus a laptop on it without any sagging. Assembly takes about five minutes because the whole unit ships pre-built; you just set it on your desk and go, in practice.

In practice, the gas spring lift is smooth, and it locks into place at five different height positions. At full extension, there’s a slight wobble if you type aggressively, but it’s nothing you’ll notice during normal work. My only real complaint is the keyboard tray—it’s just 9.5 inches deep, so my wrists rested on the edge until I adjusted my chair height.

From real-world use, Who should buy it: Anyone with a standard 60-inch desk who wants a no-fuss setup for one large monitor or two smaller ones. If you’re 5’4” to 6’0”, the height range works well.

Mount-It! Adjustable Converter

Price: around $90

If you’re on the taller side, this is the one reviewers recommend. The Mount-It! rises to 19.5 inches, which is a full 2 to 3 inches higher than most budget converters. Owner reports from people around 6’1” describe this as the only model under $100 that allows standing fully upright without hunching.

The platform is wide—about 32 inches—so you can spread out papers or use a full-size keyboard alongside your monitor. It also has a 33-pound capacity, which covers most dual-screen setups. The tradeoff is stability: at max height, the whole unit flexes more than the VIVO. You won’t drop anything, but don’t lean on it.

Assembly takes about 15 minutes and needs a Phillips screwdriver. The instructions are clear, but the bolts are small, so keep a magnet nearby.

Who should buy it: Tall users, or anyone with a higher-than-average desk who needs that extra lift range, honestly.

HUANUO Dual Monitor Converter

Price: around $70

The HUANUO is the smallest converter on this list, and that’s its superpower. It takes up barely 24 inches of desk width, which makes it perfect for cramped apartments or shared workspaces. Owner reports describe it in a tiny home office where the desk was only 40 inches wide, and it fit without forcing a coffee mug to move.

The gas spring lift is surprisingly smooth for the price. You pull the handles and it glides up with a nice, controlled motion. It holds 26.5 pounds, so it’s best for a single monitor or a laptop plus a small secondary screen. The keyboard tray is shallow at 9 inches, but the whole unit is lower, so it feels less awkward.

Downsides: The base is a bit narrow, so if you bump it hard from the side, it can shift. It’s also not great for heavy mechanical keyboards—the tray flexes slightly under pressure, in my experience.

Who should buy it: Students, remote workers in tight spaces, or anyone who moves their converter between home and office.

FlexiSpot M2B

Price: around $95

FlexiSpot is known for their full standing desks, so expectations run high for the M2B converter. It delivers. The standout feature is the keyboard tray depth—11.8 inches—which is the deepest listed under $100. Wrists stay perfectly straight when typing, and that alone saves users from the forearm ache reported with shallower trays.

The M2B holds 35 pounds and has a wide monitor platform that fits two 24-inch screens side by side. The gas spring lift is manual, meaning you pull a lever and push the platform up yourself. It’s not as effortless as some, but it’s predictable and safe—no sudden jumps, believe it or not.

The biggest downside is price. At $95, it’s at the top of the budget range, and you could almost buy a cheap full standing desk frame for that. But the build quality is noticeably better than the $70 options, with thicker steel and zero wobble at mid-height.

Who should buy it: Typists and writers who care about wrist angle, or anyone willing to spend a little more for a sturdier feel.

Ergotron WorkFit-S

Price: around $90

The Ergotron WorkFit-S is different. Instead of a tray on the desk, it clamps directly to the back edge and holds your monitor on an articulated arm. This frees up your entire desk surface, which is a game-changer if you’re working with a small table or you like to spread out paperwork, in my experience.

Setup is more involved—you attach the clamp with a hex key and then balance the monitor arm. It took me about 20 minutes, and I had to watch a YouTube video to get the tension right. But once it’s set, the movement is incredibly smooth. You can adjust the height with one finger, and there’s zero wobble at any position.

The catch is weight capacity. It holds a maximum of 20 pounds, and it only works with monitors that have VESA mounting holes (75x75 or 100x100). If you have a laptop or an all-in-one without VESA, this won’t work for you. You’ll need to buy a separate laptop tray, which pushes you over $100.

Who should buy it: People with limited desk space, or anyone who wants a clean, minimal setup with just a single monitor, believe it or not.

MOUNTUP Single Monitor Converter

Price: around $65

This is the cheapest converter in the review set that’s actually worth buying. The MOUNTUP holds up to 22 pounds, which is fine for a single 24-inch monitor or a laptop and a small phone stand. It uses a simple spring lift instead of gas pistons, and while it’s not as smooth, it gets the job done.

At full height, the platform reaches about 16.5 inches, which is adequate for most people under 5’8”. The base is solid and doesn’t slide around, even when I typed hard. Assembly is minimal—it arrives in two pieces and snaps together without tools.

The downsides are clear: no dual-monitor support, limited weight capacity, and the height adjustment feels a bit stiff at first. It loosens up after a few days of use. Also, the keyboard tray is only 8.5 inches deep, so be prepared to adjust your chair, believe it or not.

Who should buy it: Budget shoppers who only need one monitor raised, or people who want a cheap trial run before committing to a more expensive setup.

Loctek D7 Converter

Price: around $85

The Loctek D7 stands out for its built-in USB 3.0 ports and a cable management tray underneath. I plugged my keyboard and mouse into the hub, and it freed up the ports on my laptop entirely. That’s rare at this price point.

It holds 33 pounds and fits dual monitors up to 24 inches each. The gas spring lift is smooth, and the height adjustment range is solid—from 3.1 inches to 16.9 inches. The base is wide and stable, so there’s minimal wobble even at the top, in practice.

My main gripe is the size. The D7 is bulky, measuring 33.5 inches wide, and it takes over a small desk. It also has a plastic housing that feels a bit cheap compared to the all-steel FlexiSpot, though it hasn’t cracked after months of daily use.

Who should buy it: People who want extra USB ports, or anyone with a large desk (60 inches or wider) who doesn’t mind the footprint.

The bottom line

For most people, the VIVO Black Desk Converter is the best overall pick—it’s affordable, sturdy, and handles the most common setups without fuss. If you’re tall or have wrist pain, spend the extra $15 on the FlexiSpot M2B. And if your desk is tiny or you just want to try standing without a big investment, the MOUNTUP Single Monitor Converter gets you there for the price of a pizza night. Don’t overthink it; any of these will help you move more during the workday.

For a single monitor and a laptop, most of these are fine, but dual 27-inch displays plus a keyboard will push the lighter units past their limits.

Which budget converter works best for taller users?

The Mount-It!
