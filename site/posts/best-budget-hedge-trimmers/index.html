The trick is deciding how much power you actually need. If you have a few small shrubs, a manual tool is often the smartest. If you have a long fence line with overgrown greenery, a cheap electric trimmer will save you hours of back pain. Let’s break down the best budget options in both categories so you can stop staring at the overgrowth and start cutting.

What to Look For

Before you click "buy," here are the things I check on every budget trimmer to make sure it isn't a waste of money.

  • Blade Quality: Look for hardened steel or laser-cut blades. Cheaper stamped blades dull fast and tend to bend on thick branches. If it says "hardened," it’s usually worth the few extra bucks.
  • Cutting Capacity: This is the thickness of branch the tool can handle. For budget electrics, you want at least 5/8 inch. For manual shears, look for a blade gap that isn't too wide, or you’ll be crushing stems instead of slicing them.
  • Weight and Ergonomics: A heavy trimmer is a nightmare for your arms. I look for electrics under 6 pounds and manuals with a non-slip grip. If the handle feels awkward in the store, it will feel worse in the yard after 20 minutes.
  • Safety Features: A hand guard is essential on electrics to stop debris from hitting your face. On manuals, a shock-absorbing bumper is great for your wrists.
  • Power Source: Corded electrics are cheaper and lighter than battery models, but you are tethered to an outlet. Manuals are quiet and free, but they take muscle. Think about your yard size before choosing.

Fiskars 23-Inch Power-Lever Hedge Shears

Fiskars 23-Inch Power-Lever Hedge Shears are a frequent suggestion for anyone who wants to avoid the hassle of cords and batteries. Owner reviews describe use on a row of boxwoods, and the patented gear mechanism is often cited as making a difference. The design uses a lever to multiply cutting force, which means less squeezing is needed than with standard shears.

The blades are fully hardened steel, and they stay sharp for a long time. I’ve cut through branches up to 3/4 inch thick without needing to use both hands. The downside is that they are long, so they feel a bit awkward for very precise topiary work. They are best for straight lines and general shaping.

Who should buy this: If you have a small-to-medium yard with manageable hedges and you don't want to deal with extension cords, this is your best bet. It’s also perfect for anyone who hates the noise of a motor.

Scotts Outdoor Power Tools 22-Inch Hedge Trimmer

This Scotts Outdoor Power Tools 22-Inch Hedge Trimmer is a bit of a hidden gem. It’s often cheaper than the big-name brands, but it performs just as well for light-duty work. Who should buy this: This is for the casual gardener who needs to clean up the front yard a few times a year. It’s a great "set and forget" tool that won't break the bank.

Black+Decker 20V Cordless Hedge Trimmer

The Black+Decker 20V Cordless Hedge Trimmer is the most expensive item on this list, but it’s worth the stretch if you hate cords. I have this model, and the 20V Lithium battery gives me about 45 minutes of run time, which is enough to do my entire front and back yard if I’m efficient. Who should buy this: If you have a small yard without easy access to an outdoor power outlet, this is the tool for you. It’s also great for quick touch-ups and trimming in hard-to-reach areas away from the house.

Greenworks 22-Inch Corded Hedge Trimmer

For pure value, the Greenworks 22-Inch Corded Hedge Trimmer is hard to beat. It packs a 4.5-amp motor that chews through overgrown hedges without bogging down. Who should buy this: This is the best choice for homeowners with medium-to-large hedges who don’t mind using an extension cord. It offers the most cutting power per dollar.

Spear & Jackson 18-Inch Hedge Shears

Who should buy this: If you want a reliable, no-fuss manual tool for regular maintenance and you like the feel of traditional gardening tools, these are a great pick.

The Bottom Line

If I had to pick just one for most people, it would be the Greenworks 22-Inch Corded because it offers the best balance of power and price. But if you have a tiny yard and hate noise, the Fiskars manual shears are the smarter, cheaper buy. The real takeaway is that you don't need a $300 gas-powered monster to keep your yard looking sharp. A little research and a modest budget will get you a tool that lasts for years.
