15 Cheap Healthy Snacks You Can Meal Prep This Weekend

Meal prepping snacks is one of the smartest ways to save money and avoid the 3 p.m. vending machine trap. When you have a container of something good ready to grab, you are far less likely to drop five bucks on a sad, overpriced granola bar. Here are 15 cheap, healthy snack ideas you can batch out in a couple of hours this weekend.

Why Meal Prepping Snacks Saves You Real Money

Let’s be honest about the math. A single serving of chips and dip from a convenience store runs about $3.00. Do that three times a week, and you’re out nearly $40 a month. Most of the snacks below cost between $0.50 and $1.25 per serving, and many use pantry staples you already own.

The key is to buy ingredients with a low cost per pound, like eggs, oats, carrots, and beans. You aren’t buying fancy superfoods here. You are buying bulk basics and turning them into something convenient.

No-Bake Oatmeal Energy Bites

These are the workhorse of cheap snacking. They take ten minutes to mix and require zero oven time. The base recipe is forgiving, so use whatever mix-ins are on sale.

Mix 1 cup rolled oats, 1/2 cup peanut butter, 1/3 cup honey, and 1/2 cup ground flaxseed or chia seeds. Add a handful of chocolate chips or dried fruit if you have them. Stir until it forms a stiff dough. Roll into 1-inch balls and refrigerate.

This makes about 15 bites for roughly $2.50 total. They keep for a week in the fridge or a month in the freezer. Wrap them individually in plastic wrap if you want true grab-and-go convenience.

Savory Egg Muffins

Egg muffins are basically a crustless quiche baked in a muffin tin. They are high in protein, cheap, and reheat well.

Whisk 8 eggs with 1/4 cup milk, salt, and pepper. Stir in a bag of frozen spinach (thawed and squeezed dry) and 1/2 cup shredded cheddar cheese. Pour the mixture into a greased 12-cup muffin tin, filling each cup about 3/4 full. Bake at 350°F for 18-20 minutes.

Let them cool completely, then store in a zip-top bag. They last about 4 days in the fridge. To reheat, pop one in the microwave for 30 seconds. You can swap the spinach for leftover cooked veggies or canned mushrooms to change the flavor.

Roasted Chickpeas for Crunch

If you miss the crunch of chips, roasted chickpeas are the answer. A can of chickpeas costs about a dollar and yields two generous snack portions.

Drain and rinse one can of chickpeas. Pat them very dry with a clean kitchen towel. Toss with 1 tablespoon olive oil and your seasoning of choice. Try smoked paprika, garlic powder, or taco seasoning. Spread them on a baking sheet and roast at 400°F for 20-25 minutes, shaking the pan halfway. They are done when they are crunchy on the outside but not burnt.

Store them in a paper bag or a loosely covered container at room temperature for up to 3 days. They will soften if you seal them in an airtight container, so keep that lid cracked.

DIY Trail Mix Jars

Store-bought trail mix is surprisingly expensive. A small bag often costs $5. Making your own is cheaper and lets you control the salt and sugar.

Use a simple 3:1:1 ratio. Three parts cheap cereal (like plain Cheerios or store-brand corn squares), one part salted peanuts, and one part raisins or chocolate chips. Portion into small reusable containers or snack-size zip bags.

A big batch costs about $4 and makes 10 servings. This is one of the few snacks that doesn't need refrigeration, so it is perfect for desks, backpacks, and car consoles.

Yogurt and Fruit Cups

Individual yogurt cups cost about $1 each at the store. A large tub of plain yogurt costs about $4 and gives you four to five times the volume.

Portion the yogurt into small glass jars or reusable containers. Top with a spoonful of frozen berries or a chopped banana. If you need sweetness, add a tiny drizzle of honey or a sprinkle of cinnamon. Keep the fruit separate until you are ready to eat, or it will get watery.

These cups last about 3 days in the fridge. They are a great base for a quick breakfast or a filling afternoon snack.

The Hummus and Veggie Box

Hummus is cheaper to make than to buy, but even store-brand hummus is a decent deal when paired with bulk vegetables. The real trick is pre-cutting the veggies so they are as easy to grab as a bag of chips.

Cut a 2-pound bag of carrots into sticks. Slice a few cucumbers into rounds. Wash and dry a bunch of celery. Portion these into small containers with 2-3 tablespoons of hummus in the middle.

These boxes stay crisp for about 4 days. To keep the veggies from drying out, place a slightly damp paper towel in the bottom of the container before adding the vegetables.

Peanut Butter Apple "Cookies"

This is a two-ingredient snack that feels like a treat. Slice an apple into rounds, then use a small cookie cutter or a knife to remove the core from the center of each slice. Spread a thin layer of peanut butter on one side.

The fiber from the apple and the protein from the peanut butter keep you full for a long time. If you want to prep these ahead, toss the apple slices in a little lemon juice or pineapple juice to prevent browning. They are best eaten within 24 hours, so make these the night before you need them.

Freezer Smoothie Packs

Smoothies are a great snack, but buying fresh fruit every week gets pricey. Instead, buy frozen fruit in bulk bags or freeze your own ripe bananas before they go bad.

In each freezer bag, combine one sliced banana, a handful of frozen spinach, and 1/2 cup of frozen berries. When you want a snack, dump the bag into a blender with 1 cup of milk or water and blend.

These packs cost about $1 each and last for months in the freezer. They are a lifesaver when you need something cold and filling.

Hard-Boiled Egg Station

Hard-boiled eggs are the original cheap protein snack. A dozen eggs costs about $2.50, and they are portable and mess-free.

Place eggs in a pot and cover with cold water by about an inch. Bring to a boil, then turn off the heat, cover, and let them sit for 10-12 minutes. Drain and plunge into an ice bath to make them easy to peel.

Store them in the shell in the fridge for up to a week. If you want to make them more exciting, mix a little mustard, mayo, and paprika for a quick dip.

Overnight Oats in Jars

Overnight oats are more of a meal than a snack, but they work well for a filling bite between meals. The beauty is that you make four jars at once and they are ready to grab.

In each jar, combine 1/2 cup rolled oats, 1/2 cup milk or water, 1 tablespoon chia seeds (optional), and a pinch of salt. Add a spoonful of peanut butter or a handful of berries. Stir, seal, and refrigerate overnight.

They keep for up to 4 days in the fridge. If you use a wide-mouth mason jar, you can eat straight from the jar with a spoon.

The Bottom Line

Meal prepping snacks is not about being perfect. It is about having a cheap, healthy option ready when hunger hits. Start with just two or three of these recipes this weekend. You will save money, cut down on food waste, and likely eat better without even trying. The prep time is short, and the payoff is a week of stress-free snacking.
