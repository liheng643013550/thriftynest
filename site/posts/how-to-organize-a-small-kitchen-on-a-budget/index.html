How to Organize a Small Kitchen on a Budget (12 Cheap Tricks)

2026-08-18 · Organization & Storage

A small kitchen isn't a problem to fix, it's a system to design. The good news: you don't need custom cabinetry or a renovation budget. These twelve tricks cost almost nothing and will make your small kitchen feel twice as big.

1. Take everything out and sort it

Start with one drawer or one cabinet — not the whole kitchen. Empty it, wipe it, and put back only what you've used in the last month. Everything else goes in a "maybe" box. This is free, and it's the single highest-impact step.

2. Use vertical space with tension rods

A tension rod costs a few dollars and turns dead space into storage. Install one under the sink to hang spray bottles, or vertically in a cabinet to stack baking sheets and cutting boards on their sides. This one trick freed up an entire shelf in our test kitchen.

3. Group by use, not by category

Don't store all mugs together and all plates together. Store things where you use them: coffee supplies near the kettle, cooking oils and spices next to the stove, plastic wrap near where you pack leftovers. You'll move less and remember more.

4. Add a magnetic strip for knives

A magnetic knife strip on the wall or inside a cabinet door costs about $10 and gets your knife block off the counter — that's a whole counter square foot reclaimed. Bonus: knives stay sharper when they don't rattle in a drawer.

5. Stack with cheap shelf risers

Shelf risers double your cabinet space instantly. They're a few dollars each and let you use the vertical gap between shelves. Use them for mugs, canned goods, and anything that comes in short stacks.

6. Use clear bins for the chaos zones

Snack packets, cleaning supplies, plastic lids — the classic chaos zones. One clear bin per zone means you can see everything at a glance and grab without rummaging. Dollar-store bins work fine; the clear part matters more than the brand.

7. Hang pots and pans (if you can)

If you have an empty wall near the stove, a simple wall rack or even sturdy hooks turns a cabinet's worth of pots into decor. No wall space? Use a pot rack that sits on the counter's back edge, or at minimum, nest your pans with a paper towel between them to protect nonstick.

8. Store rarely used items up high

The blender you use twice a month, the slow cooker, the stand mixer attachments — they belong on the top shelf or in a cabinet you need a step stool for. Keep the prime real estate (waist-to-shoulder height) for daily items.

9. Use the inside of cabinet doors

Over-the-door organizers aren't just for closets. A slim rack inside a cabinet door holds spice jars, cutting boards, or cleaning spray bottles. Cost: under $10. Space gained: an entire shelf.

10. Add a lazy Susan for corner cabinets

Corner cabinets are black holes. A lazy Susan (about $10) turns the dead corner into usable spinning storage. Put oils, vinegars, and condiments on it so nothing gets lost behind the "wall" of forgotten jars.

11. Keep the counter 80% clear

The counter isn't a parking lot. Pick your three most-used appliances and leave them out; everything else lives in a cabinet. An 80% clear counter makes the kitchen feel bigger, cleans faster, and actually makes you cook more.

12. Label everything

Labels cost nothing if you use masking tape and a marker. Label shelves, bins, and drawer sections. Labels do two things: they keep you honest about where things go, and they make the whole kitchen look intentionally organized.

The bottom line

You can organize a small kitchen for under $50 total — or under $10 if you skip the bins. Start with the free steps (empty, sort, group by use), then add tension rods, shelf risers, and a magnetic knife strip. The goal isn't a magazine kitchen; it's a kitchen where you can find the pan, the spice, and the lid without a treasure hunt.
