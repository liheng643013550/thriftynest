How to Entertain an Indoor Cat Cheaply (12 Ideas, Most Free)

Indoor cats get bored. It’s not their fault. They have energy to burn, instincts to exercise, and a whole lot of square footage that doesn’t change. When boredom hits, you get scratched furniture, midnight zoomies, or a cat that won’t stop yelling at you.

You don’t need a $200 cat tree or a pile of fancy toys to fix that. Most of what a cat actually wants is already in your recycling bin, your pantry, or hiding in plain sight around the house. Here are 12 cheap (and mostly free) ways to keep your indoor cat entertained, starting with the easiest ideas.

Rethink the Cardboard Box

You probably already know a cat likes a box. But you can turn a plain box into a rotating attraction instead of just leaving it on the floor.

Cut a few holes in the sides of a medium-sized shipping box to create tunnels and peepholes. Toss a crumpled piece of paper inside. Then, every few days, move the box to a different room or flip it upside down. It’s the same box, but to your cat, it’s a brand-new fort.

If you want to get a little fancier, tape two or three boxes together to make a multi-room castle. Add a small towel as a bed inside. It’s free, it’s recyclable, and when the cat loses interest, you just break it down.

Build a DIY Treat Dispenser

Cats are hunters. They want to work for food, not just eat it from a bowl. You can make a simple puzzle feeder using an empty toilet paper roll.

Fold one end of the roll closed, drop a few kibbles or treats inside, then fold the other end closed. Poke a few holes in the sides with a pen. The cat will bat it around, chase it, and figure out how to shake the treats out. It’s not a perfect puzzle, but it buys you 15 minutes of quiet.

For a slightly harder version, use an empty egg carton. Put a treat in a few of the cups, close the lid, and let the cat figure out how to open it. Just use treats that are small and dry so you don’t make a sticky mess.

Create a Window Perch (Without Buying Anything)

Cats love looking outside. It’s their television. If you have a sturdy side table, a step stool, or even a stack of thick hardcover books, put it next to a window. Place a folded towel or an old pillow on top.

That’s it. You’ve made a cat perch for zero dollars.

To make it more interesting, put a bird feeder outside the window. You don’t need a fancy one. A small plastic tray with some sunflower seeds that you refill when you remember works fine. Your cat will sit and chirp at the birds for hours. If you can’t do a feeder, just leave the blinds open a crack so the cat can watch the street.

Master the Paper Bag

Cardboard boxes get the hype, but paper bags are just as good. Take a standard grocery bag, cut the handles off (so the cat doesn’t get tangled), and lay it on its side. Crumple some paper inside. That’s the whole setup.

Cats love the crinkle sound, the hiding spot, and the ambush potential. When the bag gets shredded or flattened, swap in a fresh one. Keep a stack of bags in the pantry and rotate them. It costs nothing and always works.

Turn on the Faucet (Sparingly)

Some cats are obsessed with running water. If yours is one of them, a dripping faucet can be a great 10-minute distraction. It’s not free—water costs a tiny bit—but it’s close.

If you don’t want to waste water, fill a shallow bowl or baking dish with water and drop a ping-pong ball or a plastic bottle cap in it. Place it on the floor on a towel. The cat will paw at the water and try to fish out the toy. Supervise this one so the cat doesn’t tip the whole thing over, and empty it when playtime is over.

Use the “Red Dot” Strategically

A laser pointer is one of the cheapest toys you can own, and most cats go crazy for it. You can buy a basic Laser Pointer Cat Toy for a few bucks.

But there’s a catch: a laser never gives the cat the satisfaction of catching anything. If you use it too often, it can frustrate them. The fix is simple. End every laser session by slowly moving the dot under a piece of paper or a towel, then turn the laser off. Let the cat pounce on the spot and “catch” it. Then immediately give them a treat. That tiny moment of victory makes the whole game worth it for them.

Play Hide and Seek with Treats

This is a great one for rainy days. Take a handful of dry kibble or tiny treats and hide them around the house. Under a chair, on a low shelf, inside a paper bag, on the bottom step of the stairs.

Start with easy spots so the cat understands the game. Then make them progressively harder. This engages their nose and their brain, and it’s completely free. Just avoid hiding treats anywhere the cat could knock something breakable over.

Make a Sock Catnip Toy

You don’t need to buy those expensive plush mice. Take an old clean sock, fill the toe with a handful of dried catnip, tie a knot at the top, and you’re done.

If you want to make it more interactive, tie the sock to a piece of string and drag it around the floor. That creates a prey-like motion that triggers their hunting drive. When the cat loses interest, put it in a ziplock bag. The catnip smell will stay contained, and the toy will feel “new” again next time you bring it out.

Set Up a “Cat TV” Session

Sometimes the best entertainment is just watching things move. You can create a simple moving target with your phone or an old tablet if you have one.

Search for “videos for cats” on YouTube. There are hours of footage of birds, fish, and mice running around. Prop the device against a book on the floor and let the cat watch. Just keep a hand on the device or put it in a durable case. Cats will absolutely swat at the screen.

This is a good option for when you’re busy and can’t actively play, but it shouldn’t replace real interaction. It’s a supplement, not a substitute.

Rotate the Toys You Already Own

You probably have a basket of toys right now that the cat ignores. That’s normal. Cats get bored when they see the same five toys every single day.

The fix is free. Pick up every toy you own and put them all away in a drawer or a closet. Wait a week. Then bring out only three or four toys. The cat will act like these are brand new gifts. Rotate them every week, and you’ll never have to buy more toys. This works with catnip toys especially, since the scent has time to recharge.

Use a Shoelace or Ribbon

You don’t need a fancy wand toy. Tie a shoelace or a piece of ribbon to a stick from the yard, and you have an instant interactive toy.

Drag it along the floor, flick it in the air, or hide it under a rug and wiggle the end out. This mimics a snake or a bug, which triggers their prey drive. Just make sure to supervise this one closely. You don’t want the cat swallowing a loose string. Put the stick away when you’re done.

Build a Simple Obstacle Course

If your cat is energetic, set up a free course using furniture you already have.

Place chairs upside down to create tunnels. Put a few books on the floor as stepping stones. Drape a blanket over two chairs to make a canopy. Toss a crumpled paper ball through it and let the cat chase it.

This doesn’t need to look nice. It needs to be fun. Your cat will jump, climb, and hide for a solid 20 minutes. When you’re done, just put the furniture back.

The Bottom Line

Keeping an indoor cat entertained doesn’t require a big budget. It requires a little creativity and a willingness to rotate what you already have. Cardboard boxes, paper bags, and old socks are just as good as store-bought toys. The most important thing is your time. Ten minutes of active play using a ribbon or a laser is worth more than any expensive gadget.

Try a few of these ideas this week. See which ones your cat actually responds to. Then rotate them regularly to keep things fresh. Your cat will be happier, your furniture will last longer, and your wallet will stay full.
