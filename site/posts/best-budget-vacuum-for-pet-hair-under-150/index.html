If you share your home with a shedding machine, you know the struggle: fur tumbleweeds under the sofa, a dustpan full of hair after five minutes of sweeping, and a vacuum that chokes on the first pass. You need serious suction, but you don't need to spend a fortune to get it. Here are the best budget vacuums for pet hair under $150 that actually win the fur war without emptying your wallet.

What to Look For

Worth noting, Before you click "buy," keep these criteria in mind. Not every vacuum labeled "pet" is worth your cash.

  • Strong, dedicated suction: Look for models with a motorized brush roll or a specific "pet" mode. The motor needs enough airflow to pull embedded hair out of carpet fibers, not just slide over the top.
  • Tangle resistance: This is a huge one. If the brush roll is a magnet for long hair, you'll spend more time with scissors than vacuuming. A self-cleaning or tangle-free brush roll is a massive time-saver.
  • Filtration system: Pet dander is a real allergen. A HEPA filter or a sealed system traps the microscopic stuff so it doesn't blast back into your face while you work.
  • Ease of emptying: A bin that gets clogged with hair is a nightmare. Look for a cyclonic design or a bagless system with a smooth, non-sticky interior that empties cleanly in one shot.
  • Attachments that matter: A crevice tool and a motorized turbo brush for upholstery are non-negotiable. You need to clean the couch, the stairs, and the car seat, not just the floor.

In most cases, this is the vacuum I recommend to nearly everyone on a tight budget. The Bissell CleanView Swivel Pet usually sits right around the $99 mark, making it a steal for what it does. The "Swivel" part is the secret sauce here. The steering is incredibly loose, so navigating around chair legs and under the dining table feels effortless, which is a blessing when you are chasing a dog that just shook off a layer of fur, believe it or not.

To be fair, What makes it great is the combination of the tangle-free brush roll and the scatter-free technology on hard floors. If you have a mix of carpet and tile, this thing handles the transition without launching kibble or litter across the room. It also has a one-touch dirt cup release, which sounds minor until you are holding a wad of fur the size of a hamster and need to get rid of it fast.

Downsides? The build quality is plastic-heavy, and the hose is a bit stiff. It’s not the quietest vacuum on the block either, but for the price, I can forgive a little noise.

Who should buy it? If you have a small to medium home, one or two pets, and a mix of floors, this is your best bet. It’s simple, effective, and leaves money in your pocket for a nice pet bed, in practice.

Shark Navigator Lift-Away

If you are willing to stretch your budget to the top of our range, the Shark Navigator Lift-Away at around $149 is a workhorse. The "Lift-Away" feature lets you detach the canister from the floor nozzle, turning it into a portable unit. I use this constantly for the stairs and for vacuuming out the car. It is basically two vacuums in one.

The suction power here is genuinely impressive for the price point. It has a HEPA filter and a sealed system, which is a big deal if you are allergic to dander but refuse to live in a bubble. The brush roll is easy to clean when hair wraps around it, though you will have to do it manually with scissors every couple of weeks if you have long-haired pets.

The downsides are the weight—it’s a bit top-heavy when you carry it by the handle—and the hose is a little short for tall people. You will be bending over more than you'd like, honestly.

Who should buy it? Families with multiple pets and a lot of carpet. If you need the versatility of a portable unit for deep cleaning sessions, this is the one to get.

Hoover WindTunnel 3

Hoover has been in the game for ages, and the WindTunnel 3 is proof they still know how to build a solid, no-frills machine. For about $129, you get a vacuum with three channels of suction that literally pull up dirt and hair from deep within the carpet pile. It feels robust, like it could survive being dropped down a flight of stairs.

The best part for pet owners is the large dirt cup capacity. If you have a German Shepherd or a Husky, you will not be emptying this bin mid-clean. It just keeps going. The brush roll is also on/off switchable, which is a rare find at this price. You can turn it off for bare floors to avoid scattering debris, in my experience.

The downsides are that it is bulky and not the best on hard floors—the suction is so strong that it can feel like it's sticking to the floor. Also, the attachments are stored on the back, which is fine, but they tend to rattle around.

Who should buy it? If you have wall-to-wall carpet and a big shedder, this is your machine. It’s built like a tank and holds a lot of dirt.

Bissell Pet Hair Eraser

Bissell makes a few models under this name, but the upright Pet Hair Eraser is the one to focus on. At around $129, it is designed with one thing in mind: getting hair off of things. It includes a specialized "Pet TurboEraser" tool that is motorized and works wonders on the couch. I have used it to remove embedded fur from a fabric recliner that I thought was a lost cause, and it looked brand new afterward, believe it or not.

The key feature is the tangle-free brush roll. I have tested this with my wife's long hair and our dog's medium-length fur, and it truly does not wrap around the bottom roller. This saves you a ton of maintenance time. It also has a "One-Touch" empty feature that is supposed to be mess-free, and for the most part, it works well, though you might have to pull a stray hair clump out occasionally.

Downsides? The suction on the bare floor setting is average, and it can be a little finicky to push on thick, plush carpet. It also feels a bit cheaper in the handle area than the Shark.

Who should buy it? If your primary battle is against fur on upholstery and stairs, and you hate cutting hair off the brush roll, get this one, believe it or not.

Dirt Devil Endura Max

I know, I know—Dirt Devil isn't usually the first name that comes to mind for pet hair. But the Endura Max, which you can find for under $80, is a dark horse. It’s incredibly lightweight—I can carry it up and down stairs with one finger. It has a cyclonic action that keeps the filters from clogging up immediately, which is a common issue with budget vacuums.

For the price, the suction is surprisingly strong. It won't deep-clean a shag carpet like the Hoover will, but for everyday maintenance on medium-pile carpet and area rugs, it handles the fur just fine. It also has a washable filter, so you aren't buying replacements every few months.

The downsides are clear: it feels cheap, the cord is on the shorter side (around 25 feet), and the attachments are basic. The brush roll will tangle with long hair, so you will need to clean it out, honestly.

Who should buy it? Students, renters, or anyone who needs a lightweight backup vacuum for a small apartment with one small pet. It’s a fantastic value, but you get what you pay for in build quality.

Kenmore Intuition Bagged

I have to include a bagged option because they are still the best for allergies. The Kenmore Intuition Bagged, priced near $139, is a compact canister vacuum that uses a HEPA bag. When you are dealing with pet dander, a bagged system is superior because you never have to empty a bin into the air. You just toss the bag in the trash, and all the allergens go with it.

This model is actually a "bagged canister" style, which makes it very maneuverable. The power nozzle is separate from the main body, so it follows you around easily. It has a motorized brush roll that is fantastic on carpets, and the suction control on the handle lets you dial it down for drapes or delicate fabrics, honestly.

The downsides are that you have to buy bags, which is an ongoing cost, and the wand can be a little awkward to get used to if you’ve only ever used uprights.

Who should buy it? Anyone with allergies or asthma who wants the cleanest possible emptying process. It’s also great if you have a lot of above-floor cleaning to do.

Eureka Airspeed One

Eureka is another old-school brand that still delivers value. The Airspeed One is usually around $89, putting it in the "barely over a hundred" category. It is a straight-up upright with a powerful motor that lifts embedded dirt well. It has a "no-touch" dirt cup that empties from the bottom, which is a nice touch at this price, in practice.

For pet hair, it does a solid job on low and medium pile carpets. The brush roll is stiff and does a good job of flicking hair loose, and the suction pathway is wide enough to handle clumps without clogging constantly. It is also fairly quiet compared to others in this list.

The downsides? It is not a swivel model, so steering is a bit more rigid. The filter needs regular cleaning to maintain suction, and the hose is a bit short.

Who should buy it? If you want a straightforward, reliable upright for basic fur removal without any fancy features, and you want to spend under $90, this is the pick, believe it or not.

The Bottom Line

Winning the fur war on a budget comes down to matching the tool to your home. If I had to pick just one, the Bissell CleanView Swivel Pet is the best all-around value because it covers all the bases for under a hundred bucks. However, if you have serious carpet and allergies, spending a little more on the Shark Navigator Lift-Away is worth every penny. My advice is to look at your flooring and your pet's hair type, and then pick the one that solves your biggest headache—whether that is tangled brushes or a cloud of dust when you empty the bin.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I vacuum if I have a pet?

I recommend vacuuming high-traffic areas and pet lounging spots at least twice a week. If you have a heavy shedder or a pet with long hair, you might need to do a quick pass every other day to keep the fur from building up in the carpet fibers.

Are bagless vacuums or bagged vacuums better for pet hair?

Bagless vacuums are cheaper to maintain and you can see the fur you collect, which is satisfying. However, bagged vacuums are much better if you have allergies because you never expose yourself to the dust and dander when emptying them. For pure convenience and hygiene, I prefer bagged for pets, honestly.

Why does my vacuum smell like my dog after I use it?

That smell usually comes from hair and dander that is stuck in the brush roll or the filters. I suggest emptying the dirt cup after every use, not when it is full, and washing the filters once a month. For a deep clean, sprinkle a little baking soda on the carpet before you vacuum to help deodorize the machine.
