If your electric bill keeps creeping up, the culprit is often not the big appliances you use but the gadgets that sit plugged in and quietly sip power all day. Smart plugs are the easiest fix. They let you cut power to devices completely, schedule them to run only when needed, and track what’s actually drawing energy. The good news is you don’t need to spend $30 a pop to get this done. I’ve tested several budget-friendly options, and these are the best smart plugs under $15 that actually work without fluff.

What to look for

To be fair, Before you buy, keep these criteria in mind. Not all cheap plugs are created equal.

  • Energy monitoring: If your goal is cutting waste, you want a plug that tracks usage in real time. This lets you see that your old TV in the guest room costs you $8 a month just sitting there. Not every plug under $15 has this, so check the specs.
  • App reliability: The plug is only useful if the app doesn’t crash or log you out every other day. Look for brands with stable apps like Kasa (TP-Link) or Govee. A plug with a glitchy app will end up in a drawer.
  • Schedule and timer functions: You need the ability to set sunrise/sunset times or specific on/off windows. For example, I run my coffee maker at 6:45 a.m. sharp. Basic plugs just have a countdown timer; better ones have full weekly schedules.
  • Size and physical fit: The plug block itself shouldn’t block the second outlet on your wall. Look for models with a slim profile or one that is designed to leave the adjacent socket free. This is a huge quality-of-life factor.
  • Compatibility: Make sure it works with your phone (iOS or Android) and your voice assistant, if you use one. Most work with Alexa and Google Home, but if you are deep in the Apple HomeKit ecosystem, your options under $15 are very limited.

Kasa Smart Plug HS103

From real-world use, the Kasa HS103 is the plug I recommend to friends who just want to set a lamp on a timer and forget about it. It is the most reliable budget plug I have used. The Kasa app is not the prettiest, but it has never failed to connect in the two years I have had one in my living room. Setup takes about two minutes. You plug it in, download the app, and it finds the device over Wi-Fi, believe it or not.

What makes it great is the scheduling. I have my porch light set to turn on at sunset and off at 1 a.m. The app handles the sunrise changes automatically, so I never touch it. It also works well with Alexa and Google Home if you like voice control. The physical size is compact enough that it does not completely block the second outlet, though it is not as slim as some others.

In most cases, the downside is that it does not have energy monitoring. If you want to see exactly how many watts your TV is drawing, you will need to look at the Wyze or a more expensive Kasa model. For pure on/off control, though, it is solid.

Who should buy it: Anyone who wants a set-and-forget schedule for lamps, fans, or holiday lights without spending extra on features they will not use, in practice.

Govee Smart Plug

Price: Around $10 for a single, but often sold in a 2-pack for a few dollars more

Govee is known for making cheap LED strips, and their smart plug follows the same playbook. It is minimal, white, and does the job. I bought a two-pack for my office to control a space heater and a desk lamp.

The app is straightforward. It shows you a simple on/off switch and a timer section. The scheduling is granular enough to set specific days of the week, which is helpful for my work-from-home routine. It also has a countdown timer, so I can set the soldering iron to shut off after 30 minutes if I get distracted, in practice.

The biggest downside is the size. It is a bit bulky and will cover the second outlet on most standard wall plates. I ended up using a power strip to get around this, which is fine, but it is worth knowing before you buy. The app also occasionally takes a second longer to load than the Kasa app, but it has never dropped a schedule.

Who should buy it: Budget shoppers who need a few plugs for different rooms and do not mind using a power strip to manage the physical bulk.

The Wyze Plug is the one to get if you actually want to see your energy waste. It has built-in energy monitoring, which is rare at this price point. In the Wyze app, you can see real-time wattage and historical usage. I plugged my old desktop computer into one and was shocked to see it was drawing 45 watts just in sleep mode. The plug is also tiny. It is one of the smallest on this list, so it does not block the second outlet. The app is decent, though it is primarily designed for Wyze cameras, so the smart plug controls are a bit buried in the interface.

The downside is that the Wyze app can be a little buggy with notifications. I have had a few times where the app showed the plug as offline when it was actually working fine. The scheduling, however, has been reliable. It also works with Alexa and Google Home.

Who should buy it: The energy-conscious user who wants to track usage and make data-driven changes to their habits without spending $30 on a Kasa energy monitor, in my experience.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do smart plugs save enough energy to pay for themselves?

Yes, usually within a few months. If you have a device that draws 10 watts in standby mode, that is about 2.4 kWh per month, or roughly $0.35. It does not sound like much, but if you have five or six of those devices, it adds up to $20 a year. A smart plug that cuts power completely will pay for itself in under a year.

Can I use a smart plug with a space heater or other high-wattage appliance?

Be very careful. Most smart plugs are rated for a maximum of 15 amps, but you should check the wattage rating. Space heaters often draw 1500 watts, which is close to the limit. I do not recommend using a smart plug for high-wattage devices like space heaters, air conditioners, or hair dryers. They are best for lamps, fans, coffee makers, and electronics.

Do I need a hub or a special Wi-Fi network for these plugs?

No. All the plugs listed here connect directly to your existing 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network. You do not need a separate hub. Just make sure your phone is on the same network during setup. Some routers broadcast 2.4 and 5 GHz under the same name, which can cause setup issues. If that happens, temporarily split the bands in your router settings, believe it or not.
