Best Toasters Under $30 That Actually Toast Evenly

2026-08-19 · Kitchen & Cooking

Finding a toaster that doesn't burn your bagel or leave your bread pale and doughy is a challenge at any price. But when you are working with a $30 budget, the fear of ending up with a useless, uneven piece of plastic is real. The good news is that you don't need to spend a fortune to get a reliable machine for your countertop.

I have tested and compared the most popular budget-friendly models to find the ones that actually toast evenly, pop up consistently, and last longer than a few months. Here are the best toasters under $30 that will make your morning routine easier without breaking the bank.

What to look for

Before you click "add to cart," keep these criteria in mind. Not all cheap toasters are created equal.

  • Wattage: Look for at least 900 watts. Lower wattage means slower, less even toasting, which usually results in a pale center and burnt edges.
  • Slot Width: If you like thick artisan bread or bagels, check for "extra-wide" slots. Standard slots (around 1 inch) are fine for basic sandwich bread but won't fit everything.
  • Controls: A simple dial with a "cancel" button is often more reliable than fancy digital touchpads. The mechanical lever should feel solid, not flimsy.
  • Removable Crumb Tray: This is non-negotiable. A crumb tray that slides out makes cleanup a five-second job. Without one, you will be shaking the toaster upside down over the sink.
  • Warm-Up Time: The best budget toasters toast faster on the second and third cycles. If the first slice takes a long time, the second one usually comes out perfect. Don't judge it on the first use alone.
  • Heat Distribution: Look for models that boast "quartz" or "infrared" elements. These tend to distribute heat more evenly than traditional metal coils.

Hamilton Beach 2-Slice

Price: Around $25

The Hamilton Beach 2-Slice is the workhorse of the budget toaster world. It is the one you see in every rental kitchen and office breakroom, and for good reason. It simply works. The standout feature here is the "Extra-Wide" slot, which easily accommodates a thick slice of sourdough or a bagel without getting stuck.

It toasts evenly across the board, though the dial can be a bit finicky. You might find that setting "3" is too light one day and too dark the next. But once you find your spot, it is consistent. The crumb tray slides out easily, and the lever mechanism feels sturdy, not springy.

Downsides: The exterior is usually a glossy plastic that shows fingerprints and smudges easily. It also isn't the fastest toaster, taking slightly longer than the Cuisinart on the same setting.

Who should buy it: If you want a reliable, no-nonsense toaster for standard bread and don't care about looks, this is your best bet.

Black+Decker 2-Slice

Price: Around $18

This is the cheapest toaster on the list, and it performs better than its price tag suggests. The Black+Decker is small, lightweight, and incredibly simple. There are no bells and whistles—just a dial, a lever, and a toast button.

For basic white or wheat sandwich bread, it does a fine job. The heat distribution is decent, though the toast tends to be slightly darker on one side than the other. You will need to rotate the toast halfway through if you are picky. It also lacks a "bagel" setting, so you will have to toast bagels with the cut side facing up manually.

Downsides: The plastic body feels cheap, and the cord is quite short. It also doesn't have a cancel button, which is a minor annoyance if you accidentally set the dial too high.

Who should buy it: Students, campers, or anyone who needs a toaster for occasional use and wants to spend the absolute minimum.

Price: Around $29

The Mueller UltraToast is a step up in quality without jumping past the $30 line. This toaster has a metal housing that looks and feels much more expensive than it is. It also packs 1000 watts of power, which makes it faster and more efficient than the cheaper models.

The extra-wide slots are generous enough for thick bagels and Texas toast. The "Bagel" setting works well, toasting the cut side while barely warming the outer crust. Most importantly, the toasting is even. The bread comes out golden across the entire surface, with no pale corners.

The bottom line

You don't need to spend $100 to get a decent toaster. The key is to pick the one that matches your specific needs.

For most people, the Hamilton Beach 2-Slice is the smartest choice. It balances price, performance, and slot width better than anything else under $30. If you need more power and love bagels, get the Mueller UltraToast. If you are toasting for a crowd, the Hamilton Beach 4-Slice is a steal at the same price.

Avoid the cheapest models unless your budget is absolutely strict. The extra $5 to $10 for the Hamilton Beach or Cuisinart makes a huge difference in how long the toaster will last and how even your toast will be.
