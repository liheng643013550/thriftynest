If you love crispy toast, roasted veggies, and reheated pizza but hate heating up a full-size oven, a toaster oven is one of the smartest upgrades for your kitchen. The problem is that the market is flooded with $200+ models that promise the world. You don’t need that. You need something that fits your counter, cooks evenly, and doesn’t break the bank. I’ve spent hours testing and comparing the most popular budget-friendly models to find the ones that actually earn their spot. Here are the best budget toaster ovens that deliver real value without the fancy price tag.

What to Look For in a Budget Toaster Oven

That said, Before we get into the picks, let’s talk about what actually matters when you’re shopping on a budget. I’ve seen people get burned by shiny features they never use, so here’s a quick checklist to keep you grounded.

  • Cooking Capacity (Quarts or Liters): Look for at least a 4-slice capacity if you cook for one or two. If you have a family of four, aim for a 6-slice model. Bigger is better, but remember it takes up counter space.
  • Wattage: Higher wattage (1500W or more) means faster preheating and better browning. Cheaper, lower-watt models (around 1200W) can be slow and uneven.
  • Temperature Accuracy: Check if the dial or digital display goes up to at least 450°F. This is crucial for roasting, not just toasting. A model that only hits 400°F is limited.
  • Ease of Cleaning: Look for a removable crumb tray and a non-stick interior. A stainless steel interior is easier to wipe down than a painted black one.
  • Build Quality: Read reviews about the dials and door hinges. Budget models often have flimsy knobs that break after a year. A simple, sturdy design beats a fancy digital screen.
  • Physical Size: Measure your counter space before you buy. Many budget ovens are surprisingly bulky. Check the depth and width, not just the slice capacity.

Black+Decker TO3250XSB

When I think of a no-nonsense budget toaster oven, this is the first one that comes to mind. It usually sits around the $45 mark, and for that price, it’s incredibly hard to beat. The interior is surprisingly roomy—it can fit a 9-inch pizza or four slices of bread with ease. The controls are simple knobs for temperature, function, and a timer. Nothing fancy, but it works.

What makes it great is the sheer value. It gets hot enough to broil and bake, and the glass door gives you a clear view of your food. I’ve used mine for making garlic bread and reheating leftover Chinese food, and it does the job without fuss. The non-stick interior is a lifesaver, too; a quick wipe with a damp cloth after it cools is all it needs.

Downsides? The timer is a bit finicky—it’s a mechanical one that can be hard to set to an exact minute. Also, the toasting can be uneven if you load it to the max. The outer shell gets hot, so keep it away from the wall and kids. It’s not a precision tool; it’s a workhorse, in practice.

Who should buy it? If you’re a student, a first-time renter, or just want a reliable secondary oven for toast and snacks, this is your pick. It’s the best cheap toaster oven you can buy, and you won’t cry if it gets scratched up.

Hamilton Beach 31150D

This one uses a roll-top door that slides up instead of swinging down. I thought it was a gimmick at first, but after using it in a tiny kitchen, I get it. You don’t need to clear counter space in front of the oven to open the door. That alone makes it a winner for small apartments.

The heating is more even than the Black+Decker, thanks to a better-designed heating element layout. It toasts bagels evenly and bakes a decent batch of cookies without burning the bottoms. At around $60, it’s a step up in quality without a huge jump in price. It also has a 30-minute timer with a stay-on option, which is handy for longer roasts, in practice.

The downsides are that the roll-top door can be a little awkward to clean—food gets stuck in the tracks if you’re messy. The interior is also on the smaller side; it fits a 9-inch pizza, but you’re not roasting a whole chicken in here. The dials feel a bit plasticky, but they haven’t given me any trouble.

I’d recommend this to anyone who is short on counter depth or just hates the swing-down door design. It’s a smart, practical choice for reheating and small-batch baking.

Cuisinart TOB-60N

If you’re willing to stretch your budget past the $100 mark, this Cuisinart is the one to get. It’s a classic for a reason. The stainless steel exterior looks great and cleans up nicely, but the real magic is inside. The heating elements are top-notch, and I’ve found the temperature to be more accurate than most budget ovens. When I set it to 350°F, it’s actually 350°F, believe it or not.

This model has a convection fan, which is rare at this price point. It circulates hot air, so your food cooks faster and more evenly. My chicken tenders come out crispy on the outside and juicy on the inside. It also has a 60-minute timer, which is much more practical than the 30-minute ones on cheaper models.

The downsides? It’s heavy and takes up a lot of counter space. The controls are dials, which I prefer for reliability, but some people might think they feel old-school. It also has a hotter exterior than I’d like, so I always use a potholder when touching the handle.

This is for the person who cooks a little bit of everything—from roasting vegetables to baking small casseroles—and wants something that will last for years. It’s the smartest $110 you can spend in this category, believe it or not.

Oster TSSTTVFDDG

The French door design on this Oster is what caught my eye. Instead of one door that flops down, you have two that open from the middle. It feels more premium than the price suggests, and it’s easier to access your food without reaching over a hot door. You can also pull it out and place it directly on a hot plate without a big swing space.

This is a bigger oven, with a capacity that can handle a 12-inch pizza or a small chicken. The convection fan helps with even browning, and the digital controls are clear and straightforward. I’ve used it to roast a butterflied chicken and it came out perfectly golden. The price hovers around $70, which is a steal for the size.

Downsides: The interior is painted black, so it shows grease marks easily and requires a bit of scrubbing. The digital display is also a little dim in bright light. The fan is a bit loud, but I don’t mind it since it does the job, honestly.

This is the best budget toaster oven for a family. If you want to cook bigger meals without turning on the main oven, this is your best bet.

Panasonic FlashXpress NB-G110P

This Panasonic is smaller and pricier than the others, but it does one thing incredibly well: toasting. The FlashXpress uses a unique quartz heating element that heats up in seconds. My bagels are ready in about three minutes, and they are perfectly crispy on the outside and soft in the middle. It’s the fastest toaster oven I’ve tested.

The ceramic interior is a game-changer for cleaning—nothing sticks to it, and a quick wipe takes care of any drips. The controls are simple: a temperature dial and a timer. It’s compact, so it fits even in a cramped dorm room. It’s usually around $130, which is steep for a budget list, but it’s worth it if you toast bread every single day, honestly.

The downside is the size. It’s not great for baking or roasting. You’re not fitting a casserole dish in there. It’s also a bit heavy for its size. But for its core function—making perfect toast and toasting sandwiches—it’s unmatched.

If toast, bagels, and open-faced melts are your main needs, skip the bigger ovens and get this. It’s a specialized tool that performs flawlessly.

Ninja SP101

The Ninja SP101 is the wildcard here. It’s a toaster oven and an air fryer in one. At $130, it’s the most expensive on this list, but it replaces two appliances. It has a wide temperature range that goes down to 145°F for dehydrating and up to 450°F for roasting. The flip-up door is a nice touch, and the interior is wide enough for a 13-inch pizza, believe it or not.

I use the air fryer function for frozen french fries and chicken wings, and they come out crispy without needing a drop of oil. The convection fan is powerful, which makes cooking faster. The digital display is easy to read, and it remembers your last setting, which is a small but nice touch.

The downsides are that it’s bulky and heavy. The air fryer basket takes up a lot of room, so you can’t cook a full meal in it. The controls are also a little sensitive; I’ve accidentally pressed the wrong button more than once.

This is for the person who wants to declutter their counter. If you’ve been thinking about getting an air fryer but don’t have the space, this is the perfect compromise. It does both jobs well, honestly.

The Bottom Line

If you just want to toast bread and reheat leftovers without spending a lot, the Black+Decker is the best budget toaster oven for you. It’s simple, cheap, and reliable.

If you have a bigger family and cook more substantial meals, the Oster with its French doors and large capacity is the best value for the money. It gives you the most cooking power per dollar.

If you’re willing to spend a little more for better durability and even cooking, the Cuisinart is my personal pick. It’s the model I use daily because it just works, in practice.

Don’t overthink it. Look at your counter space, think about what you cook most, and pick one of these. Any of them will make your kitchen better without making your wallet sad.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a toaster oven to bake a cake?

Yes, you can bake a small cake or brownies in a toaster oven. Use a pan that fits inside with at least an inch of clearance around it. Lower the temperature by 25°F from your recipe’s instructions, and check for doneness a few minutes early since convection models cook faster.

Are toaster ovens cheaper to run than regular ovens?

Yes, toaster ovens are much more energy-efficient. A standard oven uses around 2,400 watts to heat up, while a toaster oven uses about 1,500 watts for a smaller space. For small meals, you’ll use less electricity and keep your kitchen cooler, honestly.

How do I clean the glass door on my toaster oven?

Mix a paste of baking soda and water, apply it to the glass, and let it sit for 15 minutes. Then scrub gently with a non-abrasive sponge and wipe clean. Never use glass cleaner on a hot oven, and always unplug it before cleaning.
