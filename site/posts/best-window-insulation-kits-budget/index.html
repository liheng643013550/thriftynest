Drafty windows can turn a cozy living room into a cold spot and send your heating bill climbing. You don't need to replace your windows or hire a contractor to fix this. A simple window insulation kit is a fast, cheap fix that can make your home noticeably warmer in under an hour.

To be fair, I’ve tested several of these kits over the years in my own drafty 1920s house. Here are the best budget-friendly options that actually work.

What to Look For

Before you buy, keep these things in mind. Not all kits are the same, and the right one depends on your windows and your skill level, honestly.

If this helped, you'll also want to read Best Budget Programmable Thermostats (Set It and Save) and How to Save on Laundry Costs (Energy, Water & Detergent).

  • Type of film: Most kits use shrink film that you heat with a hair dryer to tighten. Some use a static-cling film that doesn't need heat. Shrink film is usually more invisible, but static cling is easier to remove and reuse.
  • Tape quality: The included tape is the weakest link. Look for kits with strong, wide tape that sticks to wood and vinyl frames. I've had cheap tape peel off in a week.
  • Coverage area: Kits cover different window sizes. Check the square footage. Standard kits cover about 5 feet by 7 feet, which fits one large window. Buy extra if you have big picture windows.
  • Application method: Some kits are double-sided tape only. Others use a combination of tape and a plastic film that you pull tight. For beginners, the tape-only method is less stressful.
  • Reusability: Most shrink film kits are one-season use. Static cling films and magnetic systems can be reused for years, which saves money in the long run.
  • Clarity: After shrinking, the film should be clear. Some cheaper films look wavy or cloudy. Read reviews to see how clear the final result looks.

3M Indoor Shrink Film Kit

Price: $18–$22

In practice, this is the kit I reach for first. The 3M kit comes with a roll of clear film, double-sided tape, and a squeegee. The tape is genuinely sticky and holds up well on my painted wood frames. After I heat the film with a hair dryer, it shrinks tight and becomes nearly invisible. I have these on my two largest living room windows, and you can’t tell they’re there unless you touch them.

From real-world use, the downside is the price. It’s a few dollars more than the generic brands. But the tape quality makes the difference. I’ve never had the 3M tape lift or peel, even during a week of below-freezing temps.

Who should buy it: Anyone who wants a dependable kit without fuss. If you have standard double-hung windows and want a set-it-and-forget-it solution, this is your best bet.

Duck Brand Max Strength Shrink Film

Price: $12–$15

Duck Brand is my go-to when I need to cover several windows on a tight budget. The kit includes enough film and tape for about 90 square feet, which is double the standard size. I used one box to cover four medium-sized windows in my hallway and spare bedroom. The film is a bit thinner than the 3M, but it does the job, believe it or not.

The tape is strong, and the instructions are simple. I applied it alone in about 20 minutes per window. The only thing I noticed is that the film wrinkles a bit more if you don't heat it evenly. Run the hair dryer slowly and keep it moving.

Who should buy it: Renters and homeowners who need to cover multiple windows without spending a lot. Also great for drafty basements or garages where looks don't matter as much.

Window Insulation Kit by Frost King

Price: $10–$14

Frost King has been making window insulation for years, and their kit is the no-frills workhorse. The roll of film is huge, so it’s the best value if you have a lot of windows. I used it on a rented apartment with those old, single-pane aluminum windows, and it cut the draft instantly.

The tape is decent, but it doesn't stick as well to metal frames. I had to use my own painter's tape on the edges to keep it in place on the aluminum. The film is also a bit thicker and less clear than the 3M, so it's more noticeable. But for the price, it's hard to complain, in practice.

Who should buy it: Budget-conscious folks with multiple windows. If you’re just looking to block a draft and don't care about perfect clarity, this works.

Ecoology Magnetic Insulation Kit

Price: $25–$30

This is a different approach entirely. Instead of tape, this kit uses thin magnetic strips that attach to your window frame and a sheet of film that snaps on. It’s pricier up front, but you can take it down and put it back up every season. I’ve had mine for two winters now, and it still works perfectly.

Installation is easier than tape. You peel off the backing on the magnetic strip, stick it to the frame, then press the film on. It takes about five minutes per window. The film is clear, and there's no sticky residue left when you remove it. The downside is the magnets need a metal frame or a steel strip to stick to. On my wooden frames, I had to use the included adhesive strips, which aren't as strong.

Who should buy it: Anyone who wants a reusable solution. If you hate dealing with leftover tape residue or need to remove your insulation every spring, this is worth the extra cost.

This is the thickest film Duck Brand makes. It’s about 50% thicker than their standard kit, which means it adds a bit more insulation and stands up to wear. I used this on my bedroom window, which faces the wind. The difference was noticeable right away—the room felt less cold, and I stopped hearing the wind whistle through the gap, in practice.

The thicker film is a bit harder to shrink. It takes more heat and more time to get it perfectly tight. But the finished look is just as clear as the standard film. The tape is the same strong tape from their standard kit.

Who should buy it: People in cold climates who need maximum draft blocking. Also good for windows that get a lot of direct sunlight, as the thicker film doesn't degrade as fast.

The Bottom Line

For most homes, the 3M Indoor Shrink Film Kit is the smartest buy. It has the best tape, clearest film, and a reliable track record. If you're covering a lot of windows on a strict budget, the Duck Brand Max Strength Shrink Film is a solid second choice. For those who want a reusable option, the Ecoology Magnetic Insulation Kit is worth the upfront cost.

A $20 kit won't make your home perfectly airtight, but it will cut the draft, reduce condensation, and save you a few bucks on your heating bill each month. That's a win in my book.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will window insulation kits damage my window frames?

No, not if you remove them carefully. The tape can leave a sticky residue, but it comes off with rubbing alcohol or a bit of warm soapy water. If you have painted frames, peel the tape off slowly and at an angle to avoid lifting the paint.

How long does it take to install a kit?

A standard window takes about 15 to 20 minutes from start to finish. That includes cleaning the frame, applying the tape, sticking on the film, and shrinking it with a hair dryer. Larger windows can take up to 30 minutes, honestly.

Can I open my window after installing the film?

No, not while the film is on. The film seals the window shut. If you need to open the window, you'll have to take the film down first. That's why these kits are best for winter, when you're not opening windows anyway.
