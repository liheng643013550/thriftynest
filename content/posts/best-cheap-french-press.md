A good cup of French press coffee doesn't need to cost a fortune. You can get rich, full-bodied coffee with a simple, well-made press for under $25. ## What to Look For in a Cheap French Press

Before you buy, keep these five things in mind. They make the difference between a great bargain and a frustrating gadget.

- **Filter Mesh Quality**: The mesh should be fine and tightly woven. A coarse or loose mesh lets coffee grounds slip into your cup, making it gritty and muddy.
- **Carafe Material**: Glass is standard and easy to clean, but it can break. Stainless steel is more durable and keeps coffee hot longer, though it costs a bit more. For under $25, you’ll mostly see glass, which is fine if you’re careful.
- **Plunger Seal Fit**: The plunger should slide down smoothly with a little resistance. A loose seal lets grounds escape, and a stiff one is hard to push. Check the seal is made of food-grade silicone or rubber.
- **Ease of Cleaning**: A French press with too many parts is a pain. Look for one that comes apart easily, especially the filter assembly, so you can rinse it quickly after each use.
- **Capacity**: Think about how much coffee you drink. A 34-ounce (1-liter) press makes about four cups. A smaller 12-ounce or 17-ounce press is better for one or two people.

## Secura French Press

**Price:** Around $20

The Secura is the best overall cheap French press for most people. It hits that sweet spot of price, build quality, and brew performance. The carafe is made of heat-resistant borosilicate glass, which means it won’t shatter if you pour hot water in it right after washing it with cold water.

What makes it great is the filter. It uses a double screen system—a fine mesh over a coarser support screen—which does a solid job of keeping grounds out of your mug. The frame and lid are made of polished stainless steel, so it looks nice on the counter and doesn’t feel flimsy. It comes in a few sizes, but the 34-ounce version is the sweet spot for a couple of coffee drinkers.

The downsides are minor. The handle is attached to the steel frame, and while it’s fine, it can get a little warm if you hold it for a long time. Also, the plunger can be a bit stiff when you first use it, but it loosens up after a few brews.

You should buy this if you want a reliable, good-looking press that brews clean coffee without breaking the bank. It’s a great first French press or a simple upgrade from a cheaper model you’ve been struggling with.

## Coffee Gator French Press

**Price:** Around $24

If you’ve ever broken a glass French press, the Coffee Gator is your answer. This one is made of double-wall stainless steel, so it’s basically indestructible for home use. It won’t crack, chip, or shatter, even if you knock it over in the sink.

The double-wall design does more than protect the carafe—it insulates the coffee. Your brew stays hot for about an hour, which is a big plus if you like to sip slowly or make a pot for a lazy morning. The lid is also clear, so you can watch the plunger work without lifting it up. It comes with a measuring scoop and a brush for cleaning the mesh, which is a nice touch for the price.

The downsides are that it’s heavier than glass models, and you can’t see the coffee level from the outside. You’ll need to remember how much water you poured in. Also, because it’s stainless steel, it retains heat, so the outside of the carafe can get warm to the touch.

You should buy this if you want a press that will last for years, you’re clumsy with glass, or you want your coffee to stay hot without a thermal sleeve. It’s a small step up in price but a big step up in durability.

## Frieling French Press

**Price:** Around $25

The Frieling is the premium pick at a budget price. It’s made of mirror-polished stainless steel that looks sharp and doesn’t stain or retain odors. This is the press you buy when you want something that feels expensive without the expensive price tag.

What makes it great is the triple-filter system. It has three layers of mesh that are extremely fine. This gives you one of the cleanest cups of French press coffee you can get at this price. There’s no grit at the bottom of your mug, just smooth, rich coffee. The plunger mechanism is also very smooth, and the parts are all dishwasher safe, which makes cleanup easier than most.

The downsides are that it’s a bit more expensive than the others on this list, and the polished finish shows fingerprints easily. You’ll be wiping it down often if you care about how it looks. It also doesn’t insulate as well as the double-wall Coffee Gator, so the coffee cools faster.

You should buy this if you care about the cleanest possible cup of coffee and want a press that looks great on your counter. It’s a good choice for someone who wants a long-lasting tool and is willing to spend a couple of extra bucks for better filter quality.

## Bodum Chambord French Press

**Price:** Around $20

The Bodum Chambord is the classic French press. It’s the one you see in cafes and coffee shops everywhere. It has a timeless look with its glass carafe and chrome-plated steel frame. If you want a press that looks like a French press, this is it.

What makes it great is its simplicity and reliability. The design has been around for decades because it works. The filter is a simple two-part mesh that’s easy to take apart and clean. The plunger is smooth and effective. It brews a consistent, solid cup of coffee every time. Also, because it’s so popular, you can easily find replacement parts, like a new glass carafe, if you break one.

The downsides are that the glass carafe is thin and can break if you’re not careful. The chrome frame is also not the most durable—it can scratch over time. It doesn’t have any of the fancy features like insulation or a triple filter, but it doesn’t really need them.

You should buy this if you want a no-nonsense, classic press that’s proven. It’s perfect for someone who likes the traditional look and doesn’t need extra features. It’s also a great gift for a coffee lover who appreciates a timeless design.

## OVENTE French Press

**Price:** Around $15

The OVENTE is the budget king. It’s the cheapest press on this list, but it still does the job well. If you’re on a tight budget or you’re not sure if you’ll stick with French press coffee, this is a low-risk way to find out.

What makes it great is the value. For the price, you get a borosilicate glass carafe, which is the same type of glass used in the more expensive Secura and Bodum models. It resists thermal shock, so it’s safe to use with boiling water. The plunger and filter are simple and work fine for a basic brew. It comes in multiple sizes and colors, so you can pick one that fits your kitchen.

The downsides are noticeable. The plastic lid and base feel a bit cheap, and the frame is thinner than the more expensive models. The filter mesh is also not as fine, so you might get a few more grounds in your cup. It’s not a bad press, but you can tell where the money was saved.

You should buy this if you need to save money and just want a functional press. It’s great for a dorm room, an office desk, or a first-time user who wants to try the brewing method without spending much.

## Mueller French Press

**Price:** Around $18

The Mueller is another solid budget option that focuses on a clean brew. It’s a glass carafe press with a stainless steel frame and a unique filter system that sets it apart from the other cheap models.

What makes it great is the 4-level filter system. It uses a fine mesh screen layered with a microfiber filter, which is designed to catch even the smallest coffee particles. This means you get a very clean cup with almost no sediment. The carafe is made of heat-resistant glass, and the lid is designed to be airtight, which helps keep the coffee warm a little longer. The handle is also ergonomic, making it comfortable to pour.

The downsides are that the microfiber filter needs a bit more care when cleaning, and it may need to be replaced over time. The metal frame is a bit thinner than the Secura, so it doesn’t feel quite as premium in your hand.

You should buy this if you hate gritty coffee and want the cleanest brew possible at a low price. It’s a good choice for someone who is particular about the texture of their coffee but doesn’t want to spend a lot of money.

## The Bottom Line

You don’t need to spend $50 or $100 to get a great French press. For under $25, you can get a press that brews excellent coffee and will last you for years with proper care.

If you want the best all-around press, go with the Secura. It’s the perfect balance of price, build quality, and brew performance. If you’re worried about breaking glass or want your coffee to stay hot, spend a few extra dollars on the Coffee Gator. And if you want the classic look and feel, the Bodum Chambord is a timeless choice that won’t let you down.

Pick one that fits your style and your counter space, and enjoy better coffee for less money.
