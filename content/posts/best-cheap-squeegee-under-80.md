Moving into a first apartment usually means a lot of glass: a shower door, a mirror over the sink, maybe a patio slider and a few windows that show every smudge. You want something that cleans them without streaks, and you don't want to spend real money on it. Here's the honest version of what $80 buys you in a squeegee — and why the smartest pick often comes down to whether you can get a fresh blade two years from now.

The reality check first: a squeegee is a strip of rubber on a handle. At this price you are not buying technology. You are buying a straight, stiff blade, a handle that doesn't wobble, and — the part almost nobody thinks about — a blade you can replace. Budget models usually run from about $10 to $40 for household sizes, and the ones that last are the ones with a replaceable rubber strip. What this range cannot do is magically fix a bad technique or clean a heavily scaled shower in one pass. No squeegee at any price does that. You still need to wet the glass, work top to bottom, and wipe the blade between strokes.

## What actually matters

- Replaceable blades. This is the whole ballgame. A squeegee with a swappable rubber strip can be refreshed for a few dollars instead of replaced. If you can't find the refill online in two minutes, skip the model.
- Blade material and edge. Silicone and natural rubber both work; a clean, sharp, uncut edge is what stops streaks. Owner reviews are the fastest way to learn whether a blade nicks easily.
- Handle length and reach. A short handle is fine for a bathroom mirror. For shower doors and windows you'll want an extendable or long-reach option, or a separate pole.
- Width matching the glass. A blade wider than the pane is awkward; much narrower means more passes and more streaks. Somewhere in the 10–16 inch range covers most apartment glass.
- Weight and grip. Light and stiff beats heavy and floppy. Check that the handle won't spin or flex where it meets the head.
- Parts availability as a brand habit. A maker that sells refills and clips for its squeegees usually sells them for its other gear too — which matters when you're outfitting a whole apartment on a budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do cheap squeegees have replaceable blades?

Most fixed-blade squeegees under $20 do not — you replace the whole unit. If replaceable blades matter to you, check the listing for a refill before buying.

### What's the best way to avoid streaks with a budget squeegee?

Wet the glass first, work top to bottom, and wipe the blade between strokes. Pair it with a microfiber cloth like the E-Cloth General Purpose Pack or Amazon Basics Microfiber Cloths to buff the edges.

### Why does replacement parts availability matter for a first apartment?

Because consumables — mop heads, pads, spray cartridges — are the recurring cost. A mop like the Rubbermaid Reveal Spray Mop with washable pads, or the O-Cedar EasyWring Spin Mop with cheap replacement heads, costs less to keep running over a couple of years.
