Working from a closet-sized apartment usually means the "office" is whatever flat surface you can clear off. If you want to stand for part of the day without giving up floor space or a chunk of your budget, you're in the right place. This guide is about the best cheap standing desk under $30, with a hard look at what that money really buys — and what it costs you in electricity every month.

## The reality check: what $30 can and cannot do

An electric sit-stand desk that raises and lowers at the push of a button starts well above $30. At this price you are shopping two categories: a compact non-electric desk you assemble yourself, or a desktop converter that sits on top of a table you already own.

Neither will glide up and down on a motor for $30. What you can get is a stable fixed-height surface, a manual adjuster, or a small desk that fits a corner. What you cannot get is a full-size motorized frame, memory presets, or a weight rating that handles a big dual-monitor setup.

The good news for renters: a non-electric desk uses zero electricity. That matters more than people think, and we'll get to the running cost in a minute.

## What actually matters

- Footprint, not width. Measure the gap between your bed and the wall before anything else. A 48-inch desk sounds modest until it's in a 9-by-10 room.
- Assembly and disassembly. Renters move. Look for desks that break down with a screwdriver and pack flat, not glued-and-welded units.
- Electricity draw. A motorized desk typically idles at under 1 watt and only draws real power (usually well under 100 watts) during the few seconds it moves. A manual or fixed desk draws nothing.
- Height range vs. your body. Standing desk comfort usually lands somewhere around elbow height with your forearms level. Check the stated range against your own height.
- Weight capacity. Budget desks often list a modest limit — typically 50 to 80 lbs on the smaller models. Add up your monitor, laptop, and lamp before you buy.
- Surface depth. A shallow top (under 20 inches) means your monitor sits close. Fine for a laptop, cramped for a full desktop setup.

## The monthly energy math

This is where a cheap desk quietly wins. A standing desk converter or fixed desk has no motor, so the running cost is exactly $0.00 a month.

A motorized desk is still cheap to run. Say you adjust it six times a day, and each move takes about 15 seconds at roughly 50 to 80 watts. That's a minute and a half of motor time daily — around 0.002 kWh. At a typical US rate of about 16 cents per kWh, you're looking at a few cents per year, not per month. Even a heavier dual-motor frame rarely pushes past a dollar or two a year.

So energy cost is not the reason to pick one desk over another. It's the reason a non-electric desk is a genuinely smart buy for a small rental: zero draw, zero standby, nothing to unplug when you move.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you actually get a standing desk for under $30?

You can get a compact fixed desk or a manual desktop converter in roughly the $25–$45 band, but a motorized sit-stand desk typically starts around $120. At this price you're choosing between a small desk and a converter, not a motorized frame.

### How much does a standing desk add to my electric bill?

A motorized desk usually draws well under 100 watts for a few seconds per adjustment, which works out to a few cents a year at typical US rates. A manual converter or fixed desk draws nothing at all — $0.00 a month.

### What should a renter measure before buying?

Measure the floor space between furniture and walls, since even a 48-inch desk can overwhelm a small room, and check the desk's stated height range and weight capacity against your setup. If you're buying a converter, confirm your existing table is sturdy enough to hold it.
