If you want better coffee without the machine noise, plastic pods, or the price tag of a fancy electric brewer, a pour over is the way to go. It gives you a clean, flavorful cup with total control, and you don't need to spend a fortune to get a good one. Here are the best budget pour over coffee makers under $40 that actually work well.

## What to Look For

Before you buy, keep these things in mind. They make the difference between a frustrating gadget and a daily driver.

- **Material:** For glass, look for borosilicate, which handles heat better than standard soda-lime glass. Stainless steel is practically indestructible and great for travel or camping. Plastic is fine if it's BPA-free, but it can retain odors over time.
- **Filter Type:** Metal mesh filters let more oils through for a fuller body, but they let some fine sediment into your cup. Paper filters give a cleaner, crisper taste and make cleanup a breeze. Some brewers only work with one type, so pick based on your preference.
- **Batch Size:** Are you brewing just for yourself or for a partner? Single-cup brewers are compact and fast, but if you need to make 3–4 cups at once, look for a larger capacity model like the Chemex or a bigger V60.
- **Stability and Fit:** A narrow neck on a carafe can trap heat and cause over-extraction if you don't swirl it. Make sure the brewer sits flat on your mug or carafe without wobbling. If it's a set, check that the carafe is dishwasher safe.
- **Ease of Use:** Some brewers have a steep learning curve with specific pouring techniques. Look for forgiving designs, like flat-bottomed drippers or ones with built-in ridges that help water flow evenly even if your pour isn't perfect.

## Hario V60 Ceramic

The Hario V60 is the gold standard for pour over. The spiral ridges and large single hole allow you to control the flow rate, which means you can really dial in your brew. It’s a 02 size, so it makes between 1 and 4 cups, which is perfect for most people.

**What makes it great:** The 60-degree cone shape and spiral ribs let water expand and flow evenly. It’s a simple, beautiful tool that gives you a very clean, aromatic cup. The ceramic version holds heat well, keeping your slurry at a consistent temperature.

**Downsides:** It has a learning curve. If you pour too fast or too unevenly, you’ll get a bitter or weak cup. You also need to buy V60-specific paper filters, which are slightly more expensive than standard cone filters.

**Who should buy it:** If you like tinkering with your coffee and want to improve your technique over time, this is the one. It’s a great gift for someone who already enjoys coffee and wants to go deeper without spending a lot.

## Melitta Pour-Over

This is the most budget-friendly option on the list, and honestly, it’s a fantastic starter. It’s a simple plastic cone that sits directly on your mug. No carafe, no fuss.

**What makes it great:** It’s nearly impossible to mess up. The flat bottom with three small holes slows down the water flow, which gives you a more forgiving brew. You can use standard #2 cone filters that are available in almost any grocery store. Cleanup is just tossing the filter in the trash and rinsing the cone.

**Downsides:** It’s not pretty. The plastic feels a bit cheap, and it’s limited to a single cup (about 12 oz). If you want to brew for two, you have to do it twice.

**Who should buy it:** This is perfect if you’re curious about pour over but don’t want to commit money or brainpower. It’s also great for dorm rooms, offices, or camping—anywhere you don’t want to worry about breaking something.

## Chemex Classic

The Chemex is more than a coffee maker; it’s a piece of laboratory glassware that looks beautiful on the counter. The 6-cup model is often on sale for around $35, which is a steal for the quality it provides.

**What makes it great:** It uses thicker paper filters that remove almost all oils and sediment. This results in an incredibly clean, bright, and tea-like cup of coffee. The bonded wood collar and leather tie look classy, and the carafe is large enough to serve several people at once.

**Downsides:** It’s fragile. You have to be careful washing it. The filters are proprietary and more expensive than standard ones. Also, because of the thick filter, it takes a bit longer to brew.

**Who should buy it:** If you host brunch or drink coffee with a partner, this is ideal. It’s also a great option if you prefer a lighter, more delicate flavor profile over a heavy, full-bodied one.

## Kalita Wave

The Kalita Wave is often called the “forgiving” pour over. It has a flat bottom with three small holes, which creates a uniform bed of coffee. This prevents channeling, where water carves paths through the coffee and causes uneven extraction.

**What makes it great:** It produces a very consistent, balanced cup every time. You don’t need a gooseneck kettle to get good results; a regular kettle works fine because the design handles the water distribution for you. It’s made of stainless steel, so it won’t break or crack.

**Downsides:** The wave-style filters are a bit harder to find than standard cone filters, though they are available online. The steel body gets hot during brewing, so you need to hold it by the plastic handle.

**Who should buy it:** If you want great coffee without the ritual and precision, this is your best bet. It’s also excellent for beginners who want to learn the basics without getting frustrated.

## Bee House Ceramic Dripper

The Bee House is a workhorse. It’s a ceramic dripper with a flat bottom and a single hole. It looks a bit retro, but it brews a solid, medium-bodied cup.

**What makes it great:** It’s incredibly durable. The glazed ceramic is thick and sturdy, and it doesn’t absorb heat the way plastic does. It uses standard #2 cone filters, which are cheap and easy to find. It also has a wider base, so it sits securely on most mugs without needing a special carafe.

**Downsides:** The single hole restricts flow, so if you grind your coffee too fine, the brew can stall and get bitter. It’s also a bit slower than other methods.

**Who should buy it:** Anyone who wants a simple, no-nonsense brewer that will last for years. It’s a great choice for a daily commuter mug, as it’s stable and easy to use.

## AeroPress

Okay, it’s not a classic pour over, but the AeroPress is a budget brewer that lives in the same neighborhood. It uses air pressure to push water through the coffee, which gives you a rich, concentrated cup in about two minutes.

**What makes it great:** It’s fast and clean. You press the plunger, and the coffee goes straight into your mug. Cleanup is just popping out the coffee puck and rinsing the chamber. It’s also incredibly versatile—you can make espresso-style shots, Americanos, or a regular cup.

**Downsides:** It’s plastic, and while it’s BPA-free, some people don’t like the feel. It only makes about 8 oz at a time, so it’s strictly for solo drinking. The paper filters are round and specific to the AeroPress.

**Who should buy it:** Perfect for travelers, campers, or anyone who values speed and ease above all else. If you’re a beginner, it’s almost impossible to make a bad cup with this.

## Fellow Stagg XF

The Fellow Stagg is a more premium option that sometimes dips under $40. It’s a stainless steel dripper with a flat bottom and a single hole, similar to the Kalita, but with a more refined design.

**What makes it great:** The stainless steel construction is excellent at retaining heat, which means your brew stays hot without scorching. It has a built-in handle that stays cool, and the design is sleek and modern. It’s smaller than the Kalita, so it’s perfect for a single, strong cup.

**Downsides:** It uses the same wave-style filters as the Kalita, which can be pricey. The smaller size means you can’t brew a full mug for a big coffee drinker.

**Who should buy it:** If you want a high-end feel without a high-end price, this is a great pick. It’s ideal for someone who drinks a single, strong cup every morning and wants a durable, good-looking tool.

## The Bottom Line

You don’t need to spend a lot to get a great cup of pour over coffee. If you want the classic experience and don’t mind a little practice, the Hario V60 is the best all-around choice. If you just want something that works with zero fuss, grab the Melitta. For sharing a pot with a friend, the Chemex is hard to beat. And if you want consistency without the skill, the Kalita Wave is your friend. Pick the one that fits your routine, grab some fresh beans, and enjoy the process.
