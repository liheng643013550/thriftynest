If you spend eight hours a day hunched over a laptop, you already know the drill: by 3 p.m. your neck aches, your shoulders creep up toward your ears, and your wrists start complaining. A laptop stand fixes most of that for less than the cost of a chiropractor copay.

As a rule of thumb, the catch is that "budget" stands range from $10 plastic risers to $30 aluminum models that look like they cost triple. I tested and compared the popular options under $30, and here's what actually holds up.

## What to look for

- Height and adjustability. A fixed stand lifts your screen 2-4 inches, which helps but may not get the top of the screen to eye level. Adjustable stands let you fine-tune between sitting and standing setups.
- Weight capacity and stability. Cheap stands wobble when you type on the laptop keyboard. Look for a stated weight limit (most handle 10-22 lbs) and rubber pads on the base.
- Material. Aluminum and steel run cooler and last longer. ABS plastic is lighter and cheaper but flexes under a 5-lb gaming laptop.
- Ventilation. Open-frame or slotted designs let heat escape. A solid slab of plastic can turn your laptop's underside into a griddle.
- Portability. If you move between a desk, a couch, and a coffee shop, a folding stand that fits in a laptop sleeve beats a heavy riser every time.
- Cable and accessory clearance. Some low stands leave no room for a USB hub or external drive underneath. Check the gap before you buy.

Pair any of these with a $15 wireless keyboard and mouse. Raising the screen without dropping your hands to desk level just trades neck pain for wrist pain, in my experience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I really need a laptop stand to fix neck pain?

A stand helps a lot because it raises the screen closer to eye level, which stops you from looking down all day. But it works best paired with an external keyboard and mouse so your hands stay at a comfortable height too.

### Can I use a laptop stand with a gaming laptop?

Yes, as long as the stand's weight limit covers your machine. Gaming laptops often weigh 5-7 lbs, so pick a metal stand rated for at least 10 lbs, like the Soundance or Liens models, in my experience.

### Are cheap laptop stands bad for airflow?

Not necessarily. Open-frame and mesh designs actually improve airflow by lifting the laptop off the desk. The ones to avoid are solid plastic slabs that trap heat against the bottom of your machine.
