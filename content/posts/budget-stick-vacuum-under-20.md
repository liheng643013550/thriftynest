If you live in a small rental and you're shopping for a stick vacuum under $20, there's really only one question that decides everything: do you want a cordless vacuum, or do you want a vacuum that actually cleans? At this price, you almost never get both. Nearly every sub-$20 stick vacuum is either a tiny rechargeable unit with a small battery and a narrow nozzle, or a corded "stick" that looks like a full-size vacuum but weighs and performs like a broom with a motor.

For a first-time renter in a studio or one-bedroom, that trade-off matters more than brand names, because the wrong pick ends up sitting in a closet while you borrow a real vacuum from a friend. This guide walks through what $20 genuinely buys, which real models are worth considering, and — since you're paying the electric bill now — roughly what each one costs to run per month.

## What actually matters

- **Corded or cordless.** Cordless models under $20 usually run 10–20 minutes per charge and take several hours to recharge. Corded ones run forever but need an outlet and typically have a 15–20 ft cord.
- **Watts and suction.** Budget corded sticks are often in the 200–400 watt range, while cordless units are usually lower. Higher watts don't guarantee better pickup, but very low wattage models struggle with anything beyond crumbs on hard floor.
- **Running cost.** A 300W vacuum used 15 minutes a week draws about 0.075 kWh per session. At the US average electricity rate of roughly 16–17 cents per kWh, that's around 1–2 cents per session — call it 5–10 cents a month. A cordless model that recharges for 4 hours at low draw costs a similar amount. The vacuum itself is almost never the expensive part of your electric bill.
- **Weight and storage.** A renter's closet is small. Look for something under about 5 lbs that hangs flat or stands in a corner.
- **Filtration and dust.** Cheap sticks often have thin foam filters. If you have allergies, a model with a washable HEPA-style filter is worth more than extra watts.
- **Bagged vs. bagless.** Nearly all budget sticks are bagless, which means you empty a small cup every few uses and rinse filters monthly.

## The running cost math, plainly

Here's the part people get wrong. A 300W corded stick used 15 minutes a week uses about 0.075 kWh per session, roughly 3.9 kWh a year. At around 16–17 cents per kWh, that's under a dollar a year — usually closer to 60–70 cents. A cordless model adds charging losses, but even a 4-hour charge at low draw lands in the same few-cents-per-month range.

What actually costs money is everything around the vacuum: replacement filters, batteries, and belts. Budget sticks often use proprietary filters that run $8–$15 each, and if you rinse them monthly they may last a year or more. Cordless batteries, when they fail, can cost more than the vacuum did. That's the real argument for a cheap corded stick in a small rental: nothing to replace, nothing to recharge.

## The bottom line

Under $20, buy corded. The Bissell Featherweight is the closest thing to a genuinely useful sub-$20 stick vacuum — light, no battery to die, and cheap enough that it doesn't hurt if you upgrade later. If you have carpet or a pet, skip the budget category entirely and save toward a Bissell CleanView Swivel Pet or Shark Navigator Lift-Away, because a $20 stick will frustrate you within a month.

On running cost, don't overthink it: any vacuum in this class costs well under a dollar a month in electricity. Spend your worry on filters and batteries instead, and buy the simplest machine that fits your floors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a cordless stick vacuum under $20 worth buying?

Usually not. Cordless models at that price tend to have very short runtime and take hours to recharge, so most renters are better served by a corded model like the Bissell Featherweight, which has no battery to replace.

### How much does a stick vacuum add to my electric bill each month?

Very little. A corded stick in the 200–400 watt range used about 15 minutes a week costs roughly 5–10 cents a month at typical US electricity rates, and a cordless model that recharges for a few hours lands in a similar range.

### What should I buy instead if I have carpet or a pet?

If your space has carpet or a shedding pet, save toward a full-size vacuum such as the Bissell CleanView Swivel Pet, Shark Navigator Lift-Away, or Hoover WindTunnel 3. They cost several times more than a $20 stick but handle carpet and pet hair properly.
