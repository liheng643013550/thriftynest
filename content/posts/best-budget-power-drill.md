You do not need to spend $200 to hang a shelf or build a planter box. A decent budget drill costs between $30 and $80 and will handle almost everything a beginner throws at it. This guide covers the best budget power drills right now, both corded and cordless, so you can pick the one that fits your projects and your wallet.

## What to look for

- Power source. Corded drills plug into a wall and give you constant power for $30 to $50. Cordless drills run on a battery, cost a bit more, and go anywhere. If you mostly work in a garage or near an outlet, corded is the better value. If you need to drill on a ladder or in the yard, go cordless.
- Voltage and amps. For cordless, 12V handles light tasks like assembling furniture. 18V or 20V is the sweet spot for beginners and covers drilling into wood and light metal. For corded, look for at least 6 amps.
- Chuck size. A 3/8-inch chuck fits most drill bits you will use. A 1/2-inch chuck accepts larger bits and is more versatile, but usually costs more.
- Torque and clutch settings. Torque is turning force. A clutch with multiple settings stops the drill from over-driving screws, which saves you from stripped screw heads. Look for at least 15 clutch settings on a cordless model.
- Battery platform. If you already own tools from a brand like Ryobi or DeWalt, buy a drill that uses the same battery. It saves you $40 or more on a spare battery later.
- Weight and grip. You will hold this thing overhead at some point. Anything under 3.5 pounds stays comfortable for longer jobs.
