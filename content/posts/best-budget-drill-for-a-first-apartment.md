## Best Budget Drills for a First Apartment in 2026

You just moved into your first apartment, the walls are bare, and every shelf you own is still leaning against the wall in its box. You need one drill that hangs a curtain rod without waking the baby in the next room or annoying the neighbor through a shared wall.

Here's the honest problem: this guide can't recommend a specific drill, because the honest answer for most first apartments isn't a drill at all. It's a small kit of hand tools and a few accessories that do 90% of what a first apartment actually needs, quietly, for less money, and without a single complaint from the people on the other side of the wall.

## A reality check on what this price range can and cannot do

A budget drill in the $30 to $60 range is a real tool, but it's a limited one. It will drive screws into drywall anchors, assemble flat-pack furniture, and hang light pictures. It will struggle with concrete, it will bog down in hardwood studs, and the cheaper the model, the louder and more shrill the motor tends to be.

That noise matters more than the spec sheet suggests. A budget drill under load typically runs somewhere in the 75 to 90 decibel range at arm's length, which is roughly the volume of a garbage disposal or a busy restaurant. In an apartment with thin walls, that's the sound your neighbor hears at 9 p.m. when you decide to finally hang that shelf.

What this price range cannot do: drill clean holes in brick or tile, run for an hour straight without overheating, or stay quiet. If you need those things, you're looking at a different budget entirely.

## What actually matters

- Noise level first. Brushed motors in budget drills are louder and higher-pitched than brushless ones. If sleep or shared walls are a concern, this is the single biggest factor, and it's rarely printed on the box.
- Voltage and torque. An 8V or 12V driver handles furniture and anchors. An 18V or 20V drill handles light drilling into wood. Anything under 8V is a toy.
- Battery type and runtime. Built-in batteries mean the tool dies and you wait. Removable lithium-ion batteries mean you swap and keep going.
- Chuck size. A 3/8-inch chuck takes most household bits. A 1/4-inch hex quick-change chuck is fine for driving screws but limits your drill bits.
- Weight and grip. You'll hold this thing overhead while balancing on a step stool. Under three pounds is comfortable; over four gets old fast.
- What's in the box. A drill with one bit is not a kit. Budget for bits, anchors, and a stud finder separately.

## How to keep the noise down in an apartment

Timing does more work than any tool. Most leases and most city noise ordinances treat 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. as quiet hours, so a Saturday morning project is far less likely to cause a problem than a Tuesday night one.

If you do need to drill, a few habits help. Drill at low speed with a sharp bit rather than high speed with a dull one, because the screaming sound people complain about is usually a dull bit, not the motor. Put a folded towel under the tool if you're working on a shared floor. And tell your neighbor before you start, which sounds silly and prevents most conflicts.

For anything that only needs a nail, use a nail. Hand tools are dramatically quieter than any power tool, and for picture hanging they're just as fast.

## The bottom line

For most first apartments, the honest answer is that you don't need a drill yet. You need a picture hanging kit, a hex key set, a level, and a hammer, and that combination handles hanging, furniture, and small repairs for well under $100 total, almost silently.

If you do buy a drill, buy it for the jobs hand tools genuinely can't do, keep the bits sharp, and run it during daylight. Your neighbor will thank you, and you'll still get the shelves up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I really need a drill in a first apartment?

Probably not right away. A picture hanging kit handles most wall jobs, and a hex key set handles furniture assembly, both without the noise a drill makes.

### What's the quietest way to hang pictures?

Hand tools. The OOK Brand 50 lb. Picture Hanging Kit and the Milwaukee 16 oz Framing cover most picture hanging with a hammer and a few taps instead of a motor.

### How much should I spend on first-apartment tools?

A practical starter set of a picture hanging kit, a hex key set, a level, and a hammer usually lands somewhere between $50 and $100 total, depending on which versions you pick.
