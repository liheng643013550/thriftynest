Most "save money on electricity" advice is either obvious (turn off the lights) or expensive (buy solar panels). This list is different: every tip is ranked by effort and real payoff, starting with things that cost nothing and take five minutes, and ending with the few purchases that actually earn their keep.

## Free and instant (do these this week)

**1. Turn off the vampire loads.** Electronics that sit in standby — game consoles, cable boxes, phone chargers, "smart" appliances — can draw 5-10% of a typical home's electricity. Plug clusters of them into a power strip and flip it off at night. Cost: $0. Payoff: 5-10% off your bill.

**2. Use your fridge's cold, not your faucet's hot.** Heating water is one of the biggest energy costs in a home. Rinse dishes with cold water, wash clothes on cold (modern detergents work fine), and run the dishwasher's eco cycle. Cost: $0.

**3. Air-dry half your laundry.** Every dryer load costs roughly $0.30-0.50. Hanging even one load a week saves $15-25 a year. Cost: $0.

**4. Set your thermostat 3°F cooler in winter, 3°F warmer in summer.** Every degree of setback saves about 1-3% on heating and cooling — the biggest line items on most bills. Wear a sweater. Cost: $0.

**5. Close the curtains.** In summer, block direct sun on south-facing windows; in winter, open them during the day and close them at night. Free insulation.

## Costs under $15 (buy these this month)

**6. LED bulbs everywhere.** A 9W LED replacing a 60W incandescent pays for itself in about two months and saves roughly $5-7 per bulb per year. A house with 30 bulbs saves $150-200 a year. This is the single best energy purchase you can make. [LED bulb value pack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DK6M9R4W?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__)

**7. A programmable or smart thermostat.** If you don't have one, a basic programmable thermostat ($30-60) or a [smart plug with schedule](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DJ7Q3T5K?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) for space heaters and window AC units automates the setback you were supposed to do manually. Payback: 2-4 months in most homes.

**8. Foam outlet gaskets and door draft stoppers.** A $5 pack of outlet gaskets and a $10 draft stopper for the front door plug the holes where air (and your money) leaks out. This is the cheapest insulation that exists.

**9. A low-flow showerhead.** Hot water is 15-20% of the average home's energy bill. A $15 low-flow head cuts water heating cost by 20-30% — $50-80 a year for a family. Your shower barely feels different.

**10. A smart power strip.** Unlike a regular strip, a smart strip cuts power to "secondary" outlets when the main device (like your TV) is off. Kills vampire loads automatically. [Smart power strip](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DL4W8N2C?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__)

## The bigger levers (worth planning for)

**11. Air-seal your home.** Caulk, weatherstripping, and sealing attic access costs $50-150 in materials and is the highest-ROI insulation work you can do — often 10-20% off heating and cooling. It's a weekend project with a tape measure, caulk gun, and weatherstripping.

**12. Compare electricity plans.** In deregulated states, shopping your supply rate once a year is free and regularly saves 10-20%. It takes 15 minutes. Set a calendar reminder.

**13. Wash full loads only.** Appliances use nearly the same energy per cycle regardless of load. Half-full dishwasher loads are literally half your money down the drain. Combine with #2's cold water.

## What NOT to buy (yet)

- Whole-house solar: only makes sense if you own the roof and plan to stay 7+ years.
- "Energy-saving" devices with zero math behind them: anything that plugs in to "optimize" your electricity is a scam. No exception.
- New appliances: replace only when the old one dies. A new fridge saves $50-80/year, but a working 10-year-old fridge costs you $0 to keep.

## The bottom line

Start with the five free things — they cost nothing and shave 10-20% off most bills. Then buy LED bulbs and a low-flow showerhead for under $30 combined. That's the entire "cheap" playbook: free habits first, $30 of hardware second, and everything else is optimization you can do later. Your bill will drop noticeably by the end of the first billing cycle.
