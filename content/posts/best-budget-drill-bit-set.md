Grabbing a cheap drill bit set from the checkout aisle is a gamble. You get it home, try to drive a screw into a stud, and the bit twists into a useless spiral of metal. We have all been there. It is frustrating and wasteful, but you do not need to spend a fortune to get bits that actually cut. The trick is knowing which materials, coatings, and shank types matter for the work you do around the house.

Worth noting, here is my breakdown of the best budget drill bit sets that will not let you down mid-project.

## What to look for

Before you click "buy," check for these features. They separate the tools from the trinkets, in my experience.

- **Material:** Look for High-Speed Steel (HSS) as the baseline. Cobalt (M35 or M42) is better for hardened steel and stainless, but you pay a bit more. Avoid "carbon steel" for metal work; it loses its edge fast.
- **Coating:** A black oxide or titanium nitride coating reduces friction and heat. This helps the bit bite into metal instead of just spinning and smoking. Gold titanium bits look flashy but can be brittle on cheap metal bases.
- **Shank type:** For hex shank bits (the 1/4-inch hexagon shape), they fit directly into most impact drivers without a chuck. Round shanks are fine for a standard drill. If you use an impact driver, get hex shanks; otherwise, you will be fiddling with chucks constantly.
- **Case quality:** A case that does not close properly or lets bits fall out is a dealbreaker. You will lose the 1/16-inch bit in the carpet within a week. A hard plastic case with a latch is non-negotiable for me.
- **Sizes included:** A "kit" usually means 14 to 30 pieces. Make sure it covers 1/16-inch up to 1/2-inch for twist bits. For driver bits, you need #1 and #2 Phillips, plus T15 and T25 Torx. If it only has slotted bits, skip it.
- **Price per bit:** A $15 set of 20 bits sounds cheap, but if three snap on the first use, the cost per working bit is terrible. Aim for a set that costs between $10 and $30 for decent longevity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a budget drill bit last?

With normal use on wood and soft metal, a decent HSS bit should last for a few hundred holes before needing sharpening. If you are drilling into concrete or hardened steel, expect to replace bits much sooner.

### Can I use a wood drill bit on metal?

No. Wood bits have a spur that can catch and snap on metal. Use a metal bit (HSS or cobalt) for metal surfaces. Conversely, you can use a metal bit on wood, but it will not cut as fast, in practice.

### Why do my bits keep snapping?

The most common reason is using too much pressure or running the drill at too high a speed. Let the bit do the work. Use a slow speed for metal and a medium speed for wood. Also, make sure the bit is straight in the chuck.
