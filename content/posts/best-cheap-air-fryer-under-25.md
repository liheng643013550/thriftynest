You want a small air fryer that costs less than a decent takeout meal, and you're moving into a smaller place where counter space and the electric bill both matter. The good news is that the entry-level air fryer market is genuinely good right now — the catch is that cheap and cheap to run are not the same thing. Here's how to tell them apart, and which models are worth your $25.

## The one question that decides everything

Before you look at a single model, answer this: how many people are you cooking for, and how often?

An air fryer under $25 is almost always a compact unit — typically 2 to 3 quarts, sometimes less. That's a real limitation, not a marketing one. A 2-quart basket holds roughly one generous portion of fries or two small chicken thighs in a single layer. If you crowd it, you get steamed food instead of crisp food, and you'll run two batches where you expected one.

That matters twice over in a small home. First, a small basket means more cooking cycles, and more cycles means more kilowatt-hours. Second, a compact fryer is the only kind that physically fits on a downsized counter without displacing your coffee maker. So the honest version of the question is: can you live with small batches? If yes, a sub-$25 fryer is a smart buy. If you're cooking for three, save a little longer.

## What actually matters

- Basket capacity in quarts. Under $25 you're generally looking at 1.6 to 3 quarts. Measure your counter and your appetite before you buy.
- Wattage, because it drives your bill. Most compact air fryers draw somewhere between 800W and 1,500W. A 1,000W unit running 20 minutes uses roughly 0.33 kWh — at a typical US rate of about 17 cents per kWh, that's around 6 cents a session.
- Real-world run time, not wattage alone. A lower-wattage fryer that needs 25 minutes can cost more per meal than a higher-wattage one that finishes in 15. Owner reviews are the best source for actual cook times.
- Dial vs. digital controls. Dial models are cheaper and fewer things break. Digital models usually hold temperature more consistently, which shortens cook time.
- Nonstick basket coating and dishwashing. A basket that's awkward to hand-wash gets used less. Check whether the basket is listed as dishwasher-safe.
- Footprint and cord length. In a small kitchen, depth matters more than width. A short cord limits where the unit can live.

A rough running-cost anchor: if you air fry five times a week for 20 minutes a session at roughly 1,000W, you're looking at about 1.7 kWh a week — call it 28 to 30 cents a month at typical US electricity rates. Your rate varies by state, and a 1,500W model will run noticeably higher, but the order of magnitude is small. The bigger cost is the machine itself.

## Running cost, month by month

Do the math once and it stops being abstract. Take the wattage on the label, divide by 1,000, multiply by minutes used, divide by 60, then multiply by your kWh rate.

A 1,000W compact fryer used 20 minutes, five times a week, comes to roughly 1.7 kWh weekly — about 7.3 kWh a month. At 17 cents a kWh that's around $1.25 a month. A 1,500W model doing the same work lands closer to $1.85. Neither will show up meaningfully on your bill. What does show up is cooking three batches in a small basket instead of one in a large one — that roughly triples the energy for the same meal.

So the honest ranking for a small home is: buy the smallest fryer that avoids batching, not the smallest fryer available.

## The bottom line

Under $25, your realistic options are a compact dial-controlled basket fryer like the Dash Compact Air Fryer on promotion, or a low-wattage alternative like the Bella 22-inch Electric Griddle if flat-surface cooking suits you better. Running costs for any of these are genuinely trivial — typically $1 to $2 a month at normal use — so don't let electricity fear push you into a basket that's too small. Buy for batch size and counter fit first, price second, and watch for sales rather than settling for a model that fights you every dinner.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to run a cheap air fryer each month?

At roughly 1,000W and 20 minutes a session five times a week, expect about $1.25 a month at a typical US rate near 17 cents per kWh. A 1,500W model doing the same work lands closer to $1.85.

### Is a 2-quart air fryer big enough for one person?

Usually yes for single portions, but not for a full meal with sides — you'll likely cook in batches. If you routinely cook for two, a 6-quart model like the Gourmia 6-Quart avoids the extra cycles.

### Which is cheaper to run, a small air fryer or a rice cooker?

The rice cooker, generally. The Aroma Housewares 6-Cup draws only a few hundred watts and switches to warm mode, while a compact air fryer typically runs around 1,000W or more during cooking.
