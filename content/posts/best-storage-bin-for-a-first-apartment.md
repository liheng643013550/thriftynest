## The one question that decides everything

What happens when you move it?

That's it. That's the whole test. A storage bin that survives a move, a closet re-shuffle, and two years of being overstuffed is worth ten dollars. A bin that cracks the first time you drop it on a stair landing is worth nothing, no matter how cheap it was. If you hate spending money on things that break, stop shopping by price per bin and start shopping by what the bin is made of and how much weight it can take.

## What actually matters

- Material and wall thickness. Clear polypropylene bins with flexible walls bend instead of cracking. Thin, brittle plastic shatters at the corners — that's the failure point on 90% of cheap bins. Owner reviews tend to flag this within the first few months.
- Lid design. A latching or snap lid keeps dust and pests out and stacks under weight. A friction lid that just rests on top is fine for a shelf but useless in a closet where things get piled on it.
- Actual interior dimensions. A bin labeled "large" can be 12 inches deep or 20. Measure your shelf, your closet floor, and the space under your bed before you buy anything.
- Stackability under load. If you plan to stack three high, the bottom bin carries everything above it. Look for reinforced rims and flat, interlocking lids.
- Whether it folds flat. Foldable bins collapse for moving and for storage between uses. Rigid bins don't. For a first apartment where you might move in a year, this matters more than most people expect.
- What it's made of and how long it lasts. This is the running-cost angle. A bin that lasts five years costs a fraction per year of a bin you replace every twelve months, even if the flimsy one is half the price at checkout.

## Sterilite 6-Quart Storage Bin

Rough price band: typically under $10 each, often sold in multi-packs that bring the per-unit cost down further.

This is the small-workhorse pick. Six quarts is roughly the size of a shoebox, which makes it the right call for under-sink clutter, bathroom shelf overflow, cables, cleaning supplies, and the junk drawer you swear you'll organize. The plastic is thin but flexible, so it flexes rather than cracks when you drop it.

Downsides: it's small, so buying enough of them to handle a whole closet gets expensive fast. The lids are friction-fit, not latching, so don't stack heavy things on top. Owner reviews often mention that the clear plastic scratches and clouds over time.

Who should buy it: anyone organizing a bathroom, a desk, or a single shelf who wants a cheap, replaceable, stackable unit.

## Sterilite 19849004 Storage Basket

Rough price band: usually under $10 each.

An open basket is the underrated pick. No lid means no lid to lose, no latch to break, and nothing to fight with at 7 a.m. This one works on a shelf, in a closet, or on the floor for shoes, towels, and laundry. Because it's open, air circulates — which matters for anything that might get musty.

## The bottom line

Buy fewer bins, and buy the ones that survive a move.

For most first apartments, the honest answer is a mix: a couple of latching boxes for anything you care about, a stack of cheap clear totes for bulk, and foldable cubes if you're likely to move again within two years. Skip the brittle no-name bins entirely — the few dollars you save at checkout is exactly the money you'll spend replacing them.

If you want one starting point, the IRIS USA CNL-5 Storage Box is the safest single purchase because the latching lid is the feature that actually prevents the most common failure. Build out from there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many storage bins does a first apartment actually need?

Fewer than you think. Most people do fine with a handful of latching boxes for clothes and documents, a stack of clear totes for bulk storage, and a few open baskets for daily items. Buy in stages as you find what you actually use.

### Are latching lids worth the extra cost?

For anything you care about, yes. A friction lid keeps dust off but won't survive being carried by the lid or stacked under weight. Latching boxes like the IRIS USA CNL-5 Storage Box cost more per unit but tend to last longer, which lowers the cost per year.

### What's the best bin if I'm moving again in a year?

Foldable options like the Simple Houseware Foldable Storage Cube Bins pack flat and weigh almost nothing, which makes moving day much easier. Pair them with a ClosetMaid 4-Cube Organizer if you need a frame, or use a Honey-Can-Do 5-Tier Storage Rack if you'd rather stack rigid bins vertically.
