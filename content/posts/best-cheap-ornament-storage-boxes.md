## What to look for

Before we get to the specific products, here is what I keep in mind when I’m shopping. These criteria have saved me from buying useless junk more than once.

- **Cell size:** This is the most important factor. If you have large, fragile glass balls, you need deeper cells. If you have a lot of small ornaments, you need more compartments. Look for boxes with at least 2.5-inch deep cells for standard ornaments.
- **Material:** Look for corrugated cardboard with a decent weight or polypropylene plastic. Cardboard is cheap and fine for stacking, but plastic is more resistant to moisture and pests in a garage or basement.
- **Latch security:** A flimsy tab that pops open is a disaster waiting to happen. Look for positive locking latches. I prefer boxes with a handle that is part of the lid, not just glued on.
- **Stackability:** You will end up stacking these. Make sure the box has flat sides and a sturdy lid. Flimsy lids will crush under the weight of a second or third box.
- **Versatility:** Some boxes come with removable dividers. This is great because you can adjust the space for odd-shaped items like snowmen or small figurines that don't fit standard round cells.
- **Price per unit:** Don't just look at the sticker price. Look at how many ornaments the box holds and divide the cost by that number. You might be surprised to find that a slightly more expensive box holds twice as many ornaments, making it the cheaper option in the long run.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many ornaments fit in a standard storage box?

A standard layered box like the Holiday Time option holds about 64 ornaments. A single-layer box with a larger footprint, like the STADIUM Goods, can hold up to 108. The smaller plastic cases typically hold 24 to 32 ornaments.

### Are cardboard or plastic ornament boxes better?

Cardboard boxes are usually cheaper and often have deeper, more rigid cells that are great for heavy ornaments. Plastic boxes are better for resisting moisture and are easier to see through. For storage in a damp garage, go with plastic. For a dry closet, cardboard is fine.

### Can I use these boxes for things other than ornaments?

Absolutely. I use my smaller plastic ones for storing delicate Christmas tree toppers, glass candle holders, and even string lights (I wrap them around a piece of cardboard first). The ones with adjustable dividers are also great for storing small craft supplies or jewelry.
