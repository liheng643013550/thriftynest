If you have a small balcony, a narrow side yard, or just one sunny windowsill, the whole planter box question comes down to a single decision: how much floor space can you actually give up? Everything else — material, drainage, price — follows from that. Get the footprint wrong and the best-built box in the world becomes a trip hazard you regret by August.

This guide is for the home cook squeezing herbs and a few vegetables out of a tight space on a tight budget. Every pick below stays under about $75 and is chosen first for how little room it takes up, then for what it grows.

## What actually matters

- Footprint, not volume. A 37-gallon tub sounds generous until you realize it needs roughly two feet by two feet of floor. Measure your space before you fall for a big capacity number.
- Depth for what you grow. Herbs and lettuce are happy in 6 to 8 inches of soil. Tomatoes and peppers usually want 10 to 12 inches or more. A shallow box that fits your railing may not fit a tomato.
- Drainage. Roots rot in a box that holds water. Look for pre-drilled holes or plan to drill your own. A saucer or tray keeps the mess off your floor.
- Material and weight. Resin and fabric are light and cheap; wood looks better but costs more and needs care. A filled box is heavy — a 37-gallon planter can weigh well over 100 pounds once it is full of wet soil.
- Soil is part of the budget. A cheap box plus a bag of decent mix is the real cost. Budget roughly $10 to $20 for potting mix on top of the container.
- Vertical vs. floor space. A tall, narrow box or a stackable pot uses inches of floor and feet of height. On a balcony, that trade is usually worth it.

### How much floor space does the FCMP Outdoor 37 Gallon take up?

It is a large container, so plan on roughly two feet by two feet of floor, and note that it gets very heavy once filled with wet soil. Owner reviews often mention it is best placed where you intend to keep it.
