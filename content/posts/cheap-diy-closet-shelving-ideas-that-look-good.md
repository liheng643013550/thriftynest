Moving into a first apartment together usually means one closet that has to do the work of three, and a budget that has already been spent on the deposit. The good news is that a closet can be reorganized for well under $150 in parts, and most of the projects below are quiet enough to build at 10 p.m. in a building with thin walls. The reality check comes first: at this price you are buying structure and storage, not carpentry. Expect shelf brackets, cube organizers, wire racks and bins rather than custom millwork, and expect to drill into drywall (with anchors) or lean on freestanding units. What you cannot get cheaply is a built-in look with doors and trim — that is a different budget. What you can get is a closet that photographs well, holds a real amount of stuff, and does not involve a single power saw.

One thing worth planning for before you buy anything: noise. In a first apartment, closet projects tend to happen on weeknights, and the loud parts are drilling, hammering and dragging loaded shelves across a floor. A few rules cover almost everything. Drill before 8 p.m. if your building has quiet hours, use a rubber mallet instead of a hammer where a manufacturer allows it, and put felt pads under anything that slides. Assembly of cube and wire units is usually the quiet part — it is the wall anchoring that carries through a shared wall.

## 1. A wire shelving kit as the backbone

The cheapest way to add real shelf space is a wire kit that mounts to the wall and gives you two or three tiers in one box. The [Amazon Basics Wire Shelving Kit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09W2Y51TC?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is the standard budget answer here, and owner reviews consistently mention that assembly is straightforward with basic tools. Specs show it is designed for closet and utility use, and the open wire design lets air circulate, which matters in a small apartment where damp towels and shoes end up in the same space. The trade-off at this price is finish: wire shelves are visible, so they look utilitarian unless you add bins. The loud part is anchoring it — typically four to six drill holes per unit, so do that in one session rather than spread across the evening.

## 2. Cube organizers that double as a dresser

For a couple sharing one closet, a cube unit is often the single best dollar-per-cubic-foot purchase. The [ClosetMaid 8-Cube](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BZHD3QKP?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) gives you eight compartments that hold folded clothes, shoes or bins, and it can sit on the floor inside a closet or against a bedroom wall. The [ClosetMaid 4-Cube Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07MP18DVL?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is the smaller sibling for a narrow wall or the end of a closet run. Neither is fine furniture — the panels are particleboard and the back is thin — but assembled and loaded with matching bins they read as intentional rather than cheap. Noise-wise these are the quietest project on the list: assembly is typically a screwdriver and 20 to 40 minutes per unit, with no wall drilling if you leave them freestanding.

## 3. Fabric cube bins to hide the clutter

Open cubes look messy the moment you fill them. The [Simple Houseware Foldable Storage Cube Bins](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074841G79?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) are sized to fit standard cube openings and fold flat when not in use, which helps in a small apartment. Owner reviews often mention that they hold their shape better with lighter loads, so keep heavy items like books in the lower cubes. Matching bins across a whole unit is the single biggest visual upgrade in this guide, and it costs less than one nice shelf.

## 4. A tiered shoe rack that keeps the floor clear

Shoes are what usually destroys a small closet floor. The [Honey-Can-Do 2-Tier Shoe Rack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08XTMWYQW?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a low, simple frame that fits under hanging clothes, and the [Simple Houseware 4-Tier Shoe Rack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B088QZPVPW?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) handles a shared collection without eating much depth. Both are assembly-light — typically a few minutes with the included parts — and neither requires drilling. If you are building this after 9 p.m., these are the projects to save for then.

## 5. Slim hangers to buy back hanging space

This is the least glamorous line item and the one that changes the most. The [Slim Velvet Hangers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00FXNABPI?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) are typically around a quarter-inch thick versus roughly an inch for wooden hangers, which in a 4-foot rod section can free up a meaningful chunk of hanging width depending on what you hang. Owner reviews often note that velvet grips slippery fabrics well. They are silent, they cost very little per unit in a multi-pack, and they make a mismatched closet look deliberate.

## 6. A tall storage rack for the awkward corner

Every small closet has a dead corner. The [Honey-Can-Do 5-Tier Storage Rack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJX788YC?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) uses vertical space that hanging clothes waste, and the [SimpleHouseware 6-Tier](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BNTYVFKT?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) does the same job in a taller footprint. Specs on both show open shelving, so pair them with bins rather than stacking loose items. Freestanding and drill-free, which keeps them neighbour-friendly.

## 7. A mesh shelf for the top of the closet

The strip above the hanging rod is usually wasted. The [Simple Houseware Mesh Shelf](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BTGJV9Y6?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a light wire shelf that sits or mounts high up for off-season items and luggage. Load it with lighter things — specs and owner reviews both suggest mesh shelves are not meant for heavy boxes — and it stays a quiet install.

## 8. An expandable shelf that fits without cutting

Rental closets are rarely a standard width. The [Simple Houseware Expandable Shelf](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0G67RYV8B?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) widens to bridge odd gaps, which means no measuring twice and no sawing at 11 p.m. That is the whole appeal: no cutting, no saw noise, no dust in a carpeted rental.

## 9. Baskets for the shelf you already have

Before buying anything new, use what is there. The [Honey-Can-Do Basket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CK3MTRPV?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) and the [Sterilite 19849004 Storage Basket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0044UMIJI?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) both turn a bare shelf into grouped storage — one for socks and underwear, one for chargers and small electronics. Grouping by category is what makes open shelving look tidy instead of chaotic, and it costs a few dollars a basket.

## 10. Clear bins for the top shelf

Anything above eye level should be see-through or labeled. The [IRIS USA CNL-5 Storage Box](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B084BXK6RK?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a lidded clear bin that stacks predictably, and the [Sterilite 16428012 Storage Box](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H2M2PMZZ?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is the cheaper opaque alternative for things you rarely touch. Clear bins plus a label maker is the difference between a closet that stays organized and one that reverts in three weeks.

## 11. Labels, so the system survives

The [DYMO LabelManager 160 Label Maker](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B005X9VZ70?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is the unglamorous item that keeps two people sharing a closet from undoing each other's work. Specs list a full keyboard and multiple font sizes; owner reviews often mention it is simple enough to use without reading the manual. It is also completely silent, which makes it the ideal project for the hour after quiet hours start.

## 12. Small organizers for the shelf edges and drawers

The last few dollars go furthest on small containment. The [OXO Good Grips Drawer Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CW1MF1PK?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) handles belts, socks and jewelry on a shelf or in a drawer, and the [IKEA VARIERA Box](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B011S64GAE?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a cheap, stackable catch-all for the odds and ends that otherwise migrate across every shelf. Neither needs a tool.

## The bottom line

You can rebuild a first-apartment closet for roughly the cost of a modest dinner out per item, and almost none of it requires a drill. Spend first on the cube unit and the wire kit, because those two create the actual volume; everything else is refinement. If you are assembling on a weeknight in a shared building, do the anchoring early, keep the freestanding pieces for late, and put felt pads under anything that will slide. The result will not fool anyone into thinking it is custom cabinetry, but it will hold twice what the closet held before and look deliberate doing it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a cheap closet makeover usually cost?

Most of the items here are individual purchases in the tens of dollars rather than hundreds, and the cube units and wire kit are the largest line items. Doing the whole list is not necessary — starting with the ClosetMaid 8-Cube and the Amazon Basics Wire Shelving Kit covers the bulk of the storage.

### Which of these projects is quiet enough to do late at night?

The cube organizers, fabric bins, shoe racks, hangers, baskets and the label maker need no drilling, so they are fine after quiet hours. Only the wire shelving kit requires anchors, and that is the one to schedule for early evening.

### Do I need a drill for any of these ideas?

Only for the Amazon Basics Wire Shelving Kit and optionally the Simple Houseware Mesh Shelf. Everything else — the ClosetMaid 8-Cube, the ClosetMaid 4-Cube Organizer, the shoe racks, the expandable shelf and all the bins — assembles or sits freestanding without wall holes.
