Washing your bedding is one of those chores that’s easy to put off. You spend a third of your life in bed, but out of sight, out of mind. The truth is, your sheets collect dead skin cells, sweat, body oils, dust mites, and pet dander every single night. That buildup doesn't just make your bed less fresh; it can trigger allergies and break down the fabric faster.

So, how often should you actually wash everything? The answer isn't one-size-fits-all, but there is a practical schedule that keeps your bed clean without turning laundry into a full-time job. Here is a simple routine to follow for every layer of your bedding.

## The Simple Rule for Sheets and Pillowcases

The general consensus among cleaning experts is that you should wash your sheets and pillowcases once a week. If that feels like a lot, consider this: you sweat more than you think while you sleep, even if you don't notice it. That moisture creates a perfect breeding ground for dust mites and bacteria.

Weekly washing is the sweet spot for most people. It keeps allergen levels low and prevents that greasy, stiff texture from building up on your cotton sheets. If you have pets that sleep in your bed or you eat snacks in bed, you should bump that up to every 3 to 4 days.

For pillowcases specifically, you might want to wash them even more frequently. Your face presses into the fabric for hours, transferring makeup, sunscreen, and acne-causing bacteria. If you are prone to breakouts, swapping your pillowcase twice a week is a cheap, effective skincare hack.

## When You Can Stretch It to Every Two Weeks

There is one exception to the weekly rule: if you shower right before bed, sleep alone, and don't sweat heavily at night, you can probably push your sheet washing to every two weeks. This is also acceptable during the winter months when you aren't perspiring as much.

The key is to pay attention to how the sheets feel. If they start to feel slick or look dull, that’s a sign of oil buildup. If you can smell a musty odor, you have waited too long. A bi-weekly schedule works, but it requires you to be honest about your hygiene habits. For the vast majority of people, the 7-day mark is the most reliable.

## What About the Duvet Cover and Comforter?

Here is where the schedule gets a little more forgiving. Your duvet cover is essentially a giant pillowcase for your comforter, so it collects the same dirt and skin cells as your sheets. If you use a duvet cover, wash it every two weeks. This is manageable since it’s easier to remove and wash than a bulky comforter.

If you don't use a cover and sleep directly under a comforter or quilt, you need to wash the whole thing every 2 to 3 months. To make this easier, always use a top sheet. The top sheet acts as a barrier, so the comforter itself doesn't touch your skin directly. This allows you to go longer between heavy washes, which is good because washing a bulky comforter can wear it out faster.

## The Real Deal on Pillows and Mattress Pads

You might wash your pillowcases, but do you wash the pillows themselves? Pillows absorb drool, sweat, and dead skin for years. You should wash your pillows every 3 to 6 months. Check the care tag first—most polyester and down pillows are machine washable on a gentle cycle. Dry them thoroughly on low heat to prevent mold from growing inside the filling.

Your mattress pad or protector is the workhorse of your bed. It keeps the mattress clean, so it gets dirty. Wash the mattress pad every 2 to 3 months. If you don't use a mattress protector, consider getting one. It's a cheap insurance policy that keeps your mattress from absorbing the oils and sweat that would otherwise shorten its lifespan.

## A Simple Schedule to Save You Time

To make this easy to remember, here is a quick breakdown of the schedule.

| Item | How Often | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bed Sheets | Every 7 days | Removes sweat, oil, and dust mites. |
| Pillowcases | Every 7 days (or twice a week) | Prevents acne and bacterial buildup. |
| Duvet Cover | Every 14 days | Acts as a barrier for the comforter. |
| Mattress Pad | Every 2–3 months | Protects the mattress from deep stains. |
| Pillows | Every 3–6 months | Removes allergens trapped deep inside. |
| Comforter/Quilt | Every 2–3 months | Only needs washing if protected by a cover. |

## How to Wash Bedding Without Ruining It

Washing your bedding more often doesn't matter if you are washing it wrong. Heat is the enemy of elastic and cotton fibers. Always wash your sheets and pillowcases in warm water, not hot, and dry them on a low or medium heat setting.

Avoid using fabric softener on your sheets. It coats the fibers with a waxy residue that makes them feel less breathable and reduces their absorbency. Instead, use a half-cup of white vinegar in the rinse cycle to strip away odors and soften the fabric naturally. If you want a fresh scent, add a few drops of essential oil to the wash.

For zippered mattress protectors, wash them on a gentle cycle and air dry them. The heat from the dryer can break down the waterproof membrane over time. For pillows, wash two at a time to keep the washing machine balanced. Never wash a memory foam pillow in the machine—spot clean those with a damp cloth and mild soap.

## The Bottom Line

Sticking to a strict schedule is less important than building a habit. The easiest way to remember is to pick a day of the week—like Sunday—and make it your "linen day." Strip the bed, wash the sheets, and put them back on before you go to sleep. That routine takes less than 10 minutes of active work.

For the rest of the bedding, set a reminder on your phone for the bigger items. Wash the duvet cover every other week, the mattress pad every season, and the pillows twice a year. If you do that, your bed will smell fresh, your allergies will be calmer, and your sheets will last longer. A clean bed is one of the simplest, cheapest ways to upgrade your sleep quality.
