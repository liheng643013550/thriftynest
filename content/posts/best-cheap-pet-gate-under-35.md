You're making ramen in a 12-ounce mug, balancing it on a textbook, and your roommate's puppy has just discovered that the dorm hallway is a racetrack. You need a gate that keeps the dog in one zone, fits a doorway or a dorm room opening, and costs less than a pizza delivery run — ideally under $35. The catch is that most cheap gates are wide, bulky, and designed for houses with staircases, not for 200 square feet of shared living space.

Here's the honest truth up front: the selection of pet gates that are genuinely under $35 is thin, and a few of the well-known names in this category sit right at or slightly above that line depending on the size and finish you pick. So this guide focuses on what actually fits a small space, plus a few products that are worth knowing about if you can stretch the budget or if you're really shopping for a pet rather than a baby.

## What actually matters

- Footprint and opening width. In a dorm, the gate has to span a doorway or a narrow room divider, usually somewhere between 28 and 38 inches. Measure the opening before you buy anything, and check whether the gate needs a wall cup or pressure mount — many cheap gates do not come with hardware for both.
- Mount type. Pressure-mounted gates need two flat, sturdy surfaces and no drilling, which is ideal for a rental or a dorm where you can't put holes in the wall. Hardware-mounted gates are more secure but require screws.
- Swing versus walk-through. A gate with a walk-through door is worth the extra few dollars in a small room, because you'll be stepping over it twenty times a day otherwise. A fixed panel with no door gets old fast.
- Height. Most budget gates run about 23 to 30 inches tall. That's fine for a small dog or a cat; it is not going to hold a determined 60-pound dog.
- Weight and storage. If you're moving the gate between a doorway and a closet, lighter is better. Metal gates in this range typically weigh 8 to 15 pounds.
- What it's actually rated for. Manufacturer specs list a recommended pet height or weight range, and owner reviews will tell you whether that rating holds up. Read both, because the spec is the marketing claim and the reviews are the reality.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to drill holes to install a pet gate in a dorm?

Not necessarily. Pressure-mounted gates like the North States Supergate are designed to hold in place without screws, which is why they're the usual choice for renters and dorm rooms. They do need two flat, solid surfaces to press against, so a flimsy or hollow door frame can be tricky — check that the frame is sturdy before you commit.

### How wide an opening can a cheap pet gate cover?

It varies a lot by model. The budget-friendly configurations in the North States Supergate and Summer Infant Multi-Use lines are generally built for standard doorways and narrow openings, while wider spans often require extension pieces that can push the price up. Measure your opening first, then confirm the gate's listed width range before buying.

### What's the cheapest way to keep a cat and a dog apart in a small room?

Vertical and flat space is usually cheaper than a barrier. The Amazon Basics Cat Scratching Post takes up roughly a square foot of floor, and the PetFusion Ultimate Cat Scratcher is flat enough to slide under a bed, so both give a cat a zone the dog can't reach without fencing off any floor space at all.
