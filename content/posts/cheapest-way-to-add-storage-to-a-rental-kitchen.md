Moving into a first apartment together usually means a kitchen with exactly one drawer, two cabinets, and a landlord who does not want holes in the walls. The good news is that the cheapest storage upgrades are also the least permanent. The bad news is that most people buy the wrong thing first.

The single biggest mistake buyers make is shopping for containers before they have measured anything. They grab a stack of matching bins because the set looks tidy online, get it home, and discover the bins are 2 inches too tall for the cabinet above the fridge or 4 inches too wide for the gap beside the stove. Then they either return it or cram it somewhere useless. Measure first — shelf height, shelf depth, door clearance, and the width of any gap you plan to fill. Write those numbers down and take them shopping. Every pick below assumes you did that.

There is a second mistake that matters specifically for a rental: buying a storage system whose replacement parts are hard to find. A lid cracks, a clip snaps, a shelf bracket goes missing during the move, and suddenly you own a set you cannot repair. Before you buy anything, check whether the manufacturer sells spare lids, bins, clips, and shelf pieces individually, and whether they are stocked on Amazon rather than only on the brand's own site. Filters matter too if you are adding anything that runs — a small countertop appliance or a water pitcher with a filter. Replacement cartridges that are easy to reorder keep a cheap setup working instead of turning into landfill.

With that in mind, here is what actually works.

## Measure, then buy stackable bins that sell parts separately

The workhorse of rental kitchen storage is the plain clear bin. Sterilite has sold versions of this shape for years, which is exactly why it is a good first purchase: replacement lids and matching bins stay available long after you buy, and you can add one more without hunting for a discontinued color. The [Sterilite 16428012 Storage Box](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H2M2PMZZ?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a standard clear tote that stacks with itself and shows you what is inside without opening it. Owner reviews commonly mention that the plastic is thinner than premium brands and can crack if you overload it, so keep heavy items like cans in the bottom and reserve these for dry goods, snacks, and baking supplies. Expect to pay somewhere in the low double digits per bin, and less per unit if you buy a multi-pack.

## Use a storage basket for the awkward open shelf

Rental kitchens often have one open shelf or a gap above the fridge where nothing fits. A basket turns that dead space into something usable and looks intentional. The [Sterilite 19849004 Storage Basket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0044UMIJI?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a simple open-handled basket that works for potatoes, onions, chip bags, or cleaning supplies under the sink. It is also easy to replace — the same basket has been in production long enough that reviewers report buying extras years apart and getting a match. If you want a slightly softer look, the [Honey-Can-Do Basket](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CK3MTRPV?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a fabric option that folds flat when you move, which matters if you are packing up again in twelve months.

## Add a shelf where the landlord did not

If your cabinets have a tall empty gap, a freestanding shelf insert doubles your usable space without a single screw. The [Simple Houseware Expandable Shelf](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0G67RYV8B?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) widens to fit different cabinet widths, which is useful in older apartments where nothing is square. Specs show it is meant for lightweight items — plates, mugs, spices — not cast iron. Reviewers often note that it holds up fine as long as you keep the weight balanced across both sides.

## Get the drawer under control for under twenty dollars

The classic rental kitchen has one shallow drawer that becomes a junk pile. The [OXO Good Grips Drawer Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CW1MF1PK?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a modular set you can arrange to fit odd drawer dimensions, and the individual pieces are sold separately if you want to expand later. That replaceability is the point: you are not locked into one configuration. For wider drawers, the [Simple Houseware Expandable Kitchen Drawer Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B36F899F?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) expands sideways to fill the space, and reviewers frequently mention it as a good fit for silverware plus utensils in one drawer.

## Use a rolling or tiered rack instead of a cabinet

When cabinets are full and you cannot drill, a freestanding rack in a corner is the cheapest square footage in the room. The [Honey-Can-Do 5-Tier Storage Rack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJX788YC?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) holds pantry goods, small appliances, or cleaning supplies, and the shelves are wire so you can see what is on them. Check the listed weight capacity per shelf before you load it — budget wire racks typically handle around 50 to 250 pounds per shelf depending on the model, and it varies a lot. If you only need something small for shoes or produce by the door, the [Honey-Can-Do 2-Tier Shoe Rack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08XTMWYQW?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a cheaper entry point and doubles as a produce shelf.

## Corral the pantry with containers that sell spare lids

If you are decanting flour, sugar, and cereal, buy a line that keeps selling replacement lids and gaskets. The [Rubbermaid Brilliance Pantry Containers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B1L79X41?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) are clear, stackable, and designed so the same lid fits multiple sizes in the line, which makes losing one far less annoying. The [OXO Good Grips POP Containers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TD9QT97?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) use a push-button seal and are the most commonly recommended option in owner threads about pantry moths, though they cost more per container. For a budget route, the [Progressive ProKeeper+ Bins](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FT1MYG4M?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) are built around baking staples and typically cost less per piece than the premium brands.

## Label it so you stop rebuying what you already own

This sounds like a small thing until you find three open bags of the same rice. The [DYMO LabelManager 160 Label Maker](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B005X9VZ70?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) uses replaceable label cartridges that are stocked widely, which is the whole reason it belongs on this list — you can keep buying tape for it for years. Label bins with contents and dates and you will waste noticeably less food, which is real money back.

## The bottom line

The cheapest way to add storage to a rental kitchen is not one clever product — it is measuring first, then buying a small number of items whose spare parts you can actually reorder. Clear stackable bins, one modular drawer organizer, one freestanding rack, and a label maker will fix most first-apartment kitchens for well under a couple hundred dollars. Skip anything that needs drilling, and skip any container line where you cannot find a replacement lid six months later.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need my landlord's permission to add shelves?

For anything freestanding — racks, baskets, shelf inserts, drawer organizers — no. You are not modifying the unit. The moment you drill into a cabinet or wall, you generally do, and you may be responsible for patching it at move-out. Stick to tension-based and freestanding options unless your lease says otherwise.

### How do I know if replacement parts will still be available in a year?

Look for products that have been in a manufacturer's lineup for several years and are sold as individual pieces rather than only in bundles. If the listing shows separate lids, bins, or clips for sale, that is a good sign. If a product only exists as a one-off set with no spares listed anywhere, assume you will replace the whole thing if a piece breaks.

### What should I measure before buying storage?

Shelf height, shelf depth, the width of any gap you want to fill, and door clearance if the item sits behind a cabinet door. Also measure your tallest appliance if it needs to fit underneath. Write the numbers on your phone and check them against the product's listed dimensions before you order — this one step prevents most returns.
