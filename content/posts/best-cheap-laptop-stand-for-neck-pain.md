If you're working from a laptop on the kitchen table or the corner of a couch, your neck is probably paying for it. A stand that lifts the screen to eye level is the single cheapest fix for that ache, and you don't need to spend much to get one. This guide cuts through the options so you can pick in five minutes and get back to your day.

## A Quick Reality Check on This Price Range

Cheap laptop stands — roughly $15 to $60 — do one job well: they raise the screen so you're not hunched forward. That alone takes meaningful strain off your neck and upper back.

What they can't do is fix everything. Most budget stands don't include a keyboard tray, so once the screen is up, you'll be typing on a raised laptop keyboard, which is hard on your wrists. The honest fix is a stand plus a separate keyboard and mouse, and that's where your money goes.

One more thing specific to this guide's angle: a laptop stand uses no electricity. Zero watts, zero dollars a month. The real running cost of a home office is the laptop, the monitor, the desk lamp, and the space heater under the desk. A stand is a one-time purchase that can actually lower your energy bill — because raising the laptop screen often means you stop reaching for a second monitor you didn't need.

## What Actually Matters

- Height and adjustability. You want the screen's top edge at or slightly below eye level. A fixed stand works only if its height matches your chair and desk. Adjustable stands cost a little more but fit more bodies.
- Stability at typing height. A stand that wobbles when you type is worse than no stand. Look for wide bases, rubber feet, and weight ratings that comfortably exceed your laptop (most 13–15 inch laptops weigh 3–5 pounds).
- Ventilation. Cheap plastic stands can trap heat. Open frames and aluminum let the laptop breathe. Owner reviews often mention fan noise dropping after switching to an open stand.
- Portability. If the stand lives in a bag, folded thickness matters more than anything else. If it stays on a desk, ignore portability and buy for stability.
- Keyboard compatibility. Confirm the stand's width clears your laptop's keyboard and that the lip won't block the trackpad.
- Running cost. None. A stand draws no power. The only ongoing cost is the keyboard and mouse you'll likely add.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do laptop stands use any electricity?

No. A laptop stand is a passive piece of plastic, aluminum, or steel with no power draw. The only ongoing cost in a raised-laptop setup is batteries for a separate wireless keyboard, which typically last months.

### How much should I expect to spend on a cheap laptop stand?

Most basic adjustable stands fall in the roughly $30–$45 range, while sit-stand risers run about $40–$60. Desk-clamp models like the Ergotron WorkFit-S cost considerably more, typically $250–$400.

### Will a laptop stand alone fix my neck pain?

A stand helps by raising the screen toward eye level, but you'll usually also need a separate keyboard so your hands stay at desk height. Pairing a stand with a keyboard like the Logitech K350 gives you the full fix.
