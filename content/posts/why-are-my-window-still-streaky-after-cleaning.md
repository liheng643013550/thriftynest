You just spent twenty minutes on the glass, and the afternoon sun is still showing every wipe mark like a fingerprint on a phone screen. Here's the good news: streaky windows are almost always a technique problem, a tool problem, or a cheap tool that has quietly worn out — not a sign that you did something wrong. Fixing it usually costs between nothing and about $20.

For a first apartment, this matters more than it sounds. Cheap cleaning gear fails fast, and windows are where that failure shows up first, because glass has nowhere to hide a mistake. Below are the six causes we see cited most often in owner reviews and cleaning forums, how to tell which one is yours, and what the fix runs.

## Cause 1: You Are Cleaning in Direct Sunlight

This is the single most common reason glass looks worse after you clean it.

**How to confirm it:** The streaks appear in a hazy film that seems to dry before you finish wiping, and the glass feels warm to the touch. If you clean a window that faces the afternoon sun after 2 p.m., you will almost always lose.

**The fix:** Wait for an overcast day, or clean that side of the apartment in the early morning or after sunset. Sun heats the glass and evaporates your cleaning solution in seconds, before you can buff it off. What's left behind is dissolved dirt, redeposited as a film.

**Cost:** $0. It's a scheduling change, nothing more.

## Cause 2: Paper Towels and Old Rags Are Leaving Lint

Most paper towels are designed to be absorbent, not lint-free. They shed fibers, and on glass those fibers read as streaks.

**How to confirm it:** Look at the glass at a low angle against a dark background. If you see short, fuzzy lines rather than smears, it's lint, not residue.

**The fix:** Switch to a proper microfiber cloth and use two — one damp for washing, one dry for buffing. Microfiber grabs lint rather than shedding it. A basic pack like [Amazon Basics Microfiber Cloths](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B082XSXNJ5?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is the usual starting point for a first apartment because you get enough cloths to keep a clean wash cloth and a clean dry cloth separate, which is the part people skip. If you want something with a slightly heavier weave for glass, [E-Cloth General Purpose Pack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TTCJXX4?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a step up and reviewers often mention it holds up to repeated washing better than thin budget cloths.

**Cost:** Typically $8 to $20 for a multi-pack. This is the best money you'll spend on the whole problem.

## Cause 3: Too Much Soap, or the Wrong Soap

Dish soap is excellent at cutting grease and terrible at drying clear. A few drops too many leaves a surfactant film that grabs dust within a day.

**How to confirm it:** The streaks look rainbow-ish or slightly greasy, and they reappear within 24 hours even if nobody touches the glass.

**The fix:** Use one or two drops of dish soap in a full spray bottle of water, not a squeeze. Better, use a dedicated glass cleaner or a 50/50 mix of white vinegar and water. Vinegar evaporates clean and costs pennies per bottle. If you use vinegar, don't use it on natural stone nearby — keep it on the glass.

**Cost:** Under $5 for a bottle of vinegar that will last most of a year.

## Cause 4: You Are Wiping in Circles

Circular wiping feels thorough. On glass it just smears the dirt around and leaves arcs that catch the light.

**How to confirm it:** The streaks follow curved paths, or you can see swirl marks radiating from where your hand was.

**The fix:** Work top to bottom in horizontal passes on the inside, then switch to vertical passes on the outside. That way, if a streak remains, you can tell which side of the glass it's on. Finish the edges and corners last, since that's where water pools.

**Cost:** $0.

## Cause 5: Your Squeegee Blade Is Nicked or Hardened

This is where durability really matters, and it's the failure first-time renters notice most.

**How to confirm it:** Run a fingernail along the rubber blade. If you feel a nick, or the rubber has gone stiff and shiny rather than soft and matte, it's done. A damaged blade leaves a thin line of water behind on every pass — a streak that reappears in the exact same place every time.

**The fix:** Replace the blade or the squeegee. Cheap squeegees with thin rubber typically last a season or two before the edge deforms, especially if they're stored pressed against something in a bucket. A basic [Squeegee](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F24MHHFD?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is inexpensive enough that replacing it beats fighting it. Store it hanging, not lying flat, and rinse the blade after each use — grit trapped on the rubber is what nicks it.

**Cost:** Usually under $12.

## Cause 6: Hard Water and Mineral Spots

If you live somewhere with hard water, the spots aren't soap residue at all — they're calcium and magnesium left behind when water dries.

**How to confirm it:** The spots are white, chalky, and don't wipe away with a damp cloth. They may feel slightly rough. You'll also see the same spotting on your shower head and faucets.

**The fix:** Vinegar again, or a dedicated hard-water remover. Soak a cloth in white vinegar, press it against the glass for five to ten minutes, then wipe and buff dry. Don't scrub hard — you're dissolving the mineral, not scraping it. For a small kitchen job like this, a [Pyrex Glass Measuring Cup](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01GDDXDU6?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is handy for mixing measured vinegar and water rather than eyeballing it, and it doubles as everyday kitchen gear so it isn't a single-purpose purchase.

**Cost:** Under $5 for the vinegar. If you want a dedicated remover, typically $6 to $12.

## When to Stop and Replace It Instead

Sometimes the tool has genuinely failed and no amount of technique will save it. Two honest signals:

Your microfiber cloths have gone stiff, smell sour even after washing, or leave lint — the fibers have broken down. Budget cloths often fail within 6 to 12 months with regular washing. A 12-pack like [HOMEXCEL 12-Pack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CX9718K1?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) or [Mr. Siga 12-Pack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09FX7TFJV?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a reasonable replacement tier; both are commonly cited by owners as decent for the price, though thin cloths in this range tend to wear faster than heavier ones. If you want a pack that lasts longer between replacements, [Zwipes 24-Pack](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HFXBX4N5?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) or [Scotch-Brite Large Microfiber Cloths](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0000E3QK1?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) sit a step up in durability, and [Scrub Daddy Microfiber Cloths](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FQ665JHD?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) are another option reviewers mention holding their shape well.

Your squeegee blade is nicked, curled, or no longer sits flat against the glass. Replace the whole unit rather than trying to sharpen rubber.

One more note on durability at this price point: the thing that fails first on cheap cleaning gear is almost always the soft part — rubber blades, microfiber fibers, plastic trigger assemblies on spray bottles. The handles and frames usually outlast them by years. So when you're budgeting for a first apartment, spend on the cloths and the blade, not on a fancy handle.

## Preventing This Next Time

- Clean out of direct sun, always. Early morning or evening.
- Keep two cloths per window: one damp, one dry. Never buff with the damp one.
- Use less soap than feels right. One or two drops per spray bottle.
- Buff the edges and corners last, and dry them with a separate corner of the dry cloth.
- Store your squeegee hanging, blade down, not pressed against a bucket wall.
- Wash microfiber cloths separately from towels, no fabric softener — softener clogs the fibers and turns them into lint spreaders.

## The Bottom Line

Streaky windows after cleaning are almost never about effort. They're about sunlight drying your solution too fast, soap left behind, lint from paper towels, circular wiping, a nicked squeegee blade, or hard-water minerals. Five of those six fixes cost nothing. The sixth costs under $12. Start by cleaning one window at dusk with two microfiber cloths and a single drop of soap, and see if the streaks don't simply disappear — that one test tells you which cause is yours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do my windows look streaky right after I clean them?

The most common cause is cleaning in direct sunlight, which dries the solution before you can buff it off and leaves dissolved dirt behind as a film. The second most common is using paper towels or worn rags that shed lint onto the glass.

### Do microfiber cloths wear out and start leaving streaks?

Yes. Cloths that have gone stiff, smell sour after washing, or leave lint have broken down and should be replaced. Budget cloths often fail within 6 to 12 months of regular washing, and fabric softener speeds that up because it clogs the fibers.

### What is the cheapest fix for hard-water spots on glass?

White vinegar, typically under $5 a bottle. Soak a cloth in it, press it against the glass for five to ten minutes, then wipe and buff dry — you're dissolving the mineral rather than scrubbing it off.
