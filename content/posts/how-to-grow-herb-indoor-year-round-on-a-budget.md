Growing herbs indoors year round is genuinely doable in a small space, but the budget version looks different from what social media suggests. A sunny windowsill and a $15 setup can keep you in basil and parsley for months; a full grow-light shelf can quietly add $8–$20 a month to your electric bill depending on what you buy and how long you run it. This guide is built around that second number — the running cost — because when you're downsizing, the upfront price is only half the story.

## The Reality Check: What a Budget Setup Can and Cannot Do

A cheap indoor herb setup can absolutely keep hardy herbs alive and productive through winter. What it usually cannot do is grow light-hungry herbs like basil or dill at full summer speed on a dark north-facing windowsill in January. That's a physics problem, not a shopping problem.

Here's the honest split:

- **Works on a budget:** parsley, chives, mint, thyme, oregano, and rosemary in a bright window, especially with a modest supplemental light.
- **Struggles without real light:** basil, dill, cilantro, and anything you're trying to harvest heavily through a dark winter.
- **Never worth it indoors:** large plants, big harvests, or anything you're growing to save serious grocery money. The savings are real but modest — think a few dollars a month in herbs, not a grocery bill.

The running cost is the part people skip. A single 9-watt LED bulb run 12 hours a day costs roughly a dollar or two a month at typical US electricity rates (around 13–17 cents per kWh). A 45-watt full-spectrum panel run 14 hours a day lands closer to $2.50–$4 a month. A multi-shelf fluorescent rig can push past $10. None of that is ruinous — but it's the difference between a hobby that pays for itself and one that doesn't.

## Start With Light, Because That's Where the Money Goes

Light is the single biggest driver of both success and cost. Before you buy anything, audit your windows.

A south-facing window gives you the most usable light. East is decent for morning sun. West works but runs hot in summer. North is the hardest — plan on supplemental light there.

If you have a genuinely bright south window, you may need no light at all for parsley, chives, and mint. That's the cheapest possible setup: zero running cost beyond your normal lighting.

If you need a light, buy an LED, not fluorescent. LEDs convert more electricity into usable light and run cooler, which matters in a small room. Look for:

- **Wattage drawn from the wall:** 15–45 watts is plenty for a windowsill's worth of herbs.
- **Color temperature:** 5000–6500K ("daylight") works well for leafy growth.
- **Timer:** a cheap mechanical outlet timer (usually $8–$15) pays for itself by stopping you from leaving lights on all night.

Run lights 12–14 hours a day, not 24. Plants need a dark period, and you're paying for every hour.

## Pick Herbs That Forgive a Budget Setup

Spend your money on plants that tolerate indoor conditions instead of fighting the ones that don't. The specs and grower guidance are consistent on this:

- **Thyme, oregano, rosemary, and sage:** Mediterranean, want gritty soil and less water. Rosemary is the fussiest about light.
- **Parsley and chives:** the most forgiving windowsill herbs. Chives will sulk in deep shade but rarely die.
- **Mint:** vigorous to a fault. Give it its own pot or it will take over.
- **Basil:** wants warmth (65–75°F) and strong light. Expect slower growth in winter no matter what you do.

Buy small starter plants rather than seed if you want a fast start — a 4-inch pot typically runs $3–$6 at a garden center, versus $2–$4 for a seed packet that may take weeks to size up. Seed is cheaper per plant if you're patient; starts are cheaper per *result*.

## Containers, Soil, and Drainage on a Small Budget

You don't need special pots. Any container with a drainage hole works, and a drainage hole is non-negotiable — root rot is the number-one killer of budget indoor herbs.

What to buy:

- **Pots:** 6-inch pots for individual herbs, typically $2–$5 each. A saucer underneath protects your windowsill.
- **Soil:** a light, well-draining potting mix, not garden soil. A 16-quart bag of [Miracle-Gro Potting Mix](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B087F7GNFG?tag=__AMAZON_TAG__) is a common, affordable choice and enough to fill several small pots.
- **A watering can with a narrow spout:** makes it easy to water without splashing. A small 1–2 quart can is plenty and usually under $15.

Skip self-watering gadgets and moisture meters at the budget end — owner reviews on cheap meters are notoriously inconsistent. The finger test (stick a finger an inch into the soil) is free and more reliable.

## Watering and Feeding Without Wasting Money

Overwatering is the most common indoor herb mistake, and it's also the cheapest one to avoid. Water when the top inch of soil feels dry, then water until it runs out the bottom. Empty the saucer.

Fertilizer matters less than light, but a little helps. A balanced liquid fertilizer diluted to half strength every 3–4 weeks through winter is usually enough — more is not better, and overfeeding in low light causes weak, leggy growth. A small bottle of liquid fertilizer typically costs $8–$12 and lasts a season or more.

If you're moving herbs from a sunny windowsill to a darker spot, expect to water less. Cool rooms slow growth and reduce water use.

## Keeping the Monthly Cost Down

Here's where a downsized home actually helps: less space means less to light and heat. A few practical moves:

- **Group plants together** near your brightest window. It concentrates light and humidity.
- **Use a timer** so lights run exactly 12–14 hours, not "whenever I remember."
- **Choose LED over fluorescent** every time. The upfront cost is similar now, and the running cost is lower.
- **Reflect light** with a plain white wall or a sheet of white cardboard behind the pots. Free.
- **Rotate pots weekly** so all sides get light and plants don't stretch toward the window.

A realistic monthly running cost for a small windowsill setup with one modest LED and a timer is typically **$1–$4**. A larger multi-pot shelf with a 45-watt panel lands around **$3–$6**. Anything much above that means you're lighting more than you're harvesting.

## Common Mistakes That Cost You Money

- **Buying a big grow light for three pots.** You'll pay for light you don't need.
- **Skipping drainage.** Root rot means replacing plants, which is the most expensive outcome.
- **Running lights 24/7.** Costs more and stresses plants.
- **Overwatering in low light.** The soil stays wet, roots suffocate, and you buy new plants.
- **Starting from seed in December.** Slow, cheap per plant, but frustrating without strong light.

## The Bottom Line

Growing herbs indoors year round on a budget is mostly a light-and-discipline game. A bright window plus a small LED and a timer gets you most of the way for a few dollars a month in electricity. Spend your upfront money on decent potting mix, pots with drainage, and a timer — not on gadgets. Pick forgiving herbs, water less than you think, and treat the running cost as the real budget line.
