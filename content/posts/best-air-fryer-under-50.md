You don’t need to spend a hundred bucks to get crispy fries and perfectly roasted veggies. The air fryer market has gotten incredibly competitive, and some of the best budget models are shockingly good. Reviews and spec sheets cover seven models under $50 to identify the ones that cook evenly, don't scream like a jet engine, and are easy to clean.

## What to look for

Before you buy, here is what actually matters at this price point.

- **Wattage (Power):** Look for at least 1400 watts. Less than that, and you will notice longer cook times and less crispy results, especially for frozen foods.
- **Basket Size (Quarts):** For a household of 1-2 people, 3 to 4 quarts is the sweet spot. It fits a small chicken or a decent batch of fries without taking up half your counter.
- **Temperature Range:** Make sure it goes down to at least 180°F. This is crucial for dehydrating or reheating leftovers without burning them.
- **Controls:** Digital touchscreens look nice, but physical dials or buttons are often more reliable on budget models. Look for a simple, intuitive interface.
- **Dishwasher Safety:** Check if the basket and crisper plate are actually dishwasher safe. Some say they are but warp easily. Non-stick coating is a must for easy cleanup.
- **Build Quality:** At this price, plastic is expected, but it shouldn't feel flimsy. Check that the basket slides in and out smoothly and locks securely.

## Cosori Pro LE

The Cosori Pro LE is the best air fryer you can get for around $50. It consistently outperforms other budget models in published tests, mostly because of its 1500-watt heating element and a fan that distributes heat more evenly than most. Owner reports describe frozen chicken wings coming out unbelievably crispy on the outside and juicy inside, with no cold spots.

The digital touchscreen is a cut above what you usually see at this price. It has a nice shake reminder that actually works, and the interface is simple to navigate. One of the standout features is the ability to connect it to the VeSync app, which gives you access to hundreds of recipes. It's a gimmick for some, but it is genuinely useful for finding cook times.

The downside is the 4-quart basket. It's fine for two people, but you will be cooking in batches for a family of four. The non-stick coating is good, but we recommend washing the basket by hand to make it last longer, even though it says dishwasher safe.

**Who should buy it:** This is for the person who wants the most reliable, best-cooking air fryer without paying premium prices. It is the safest bet.

## Instant Pot Vortex 4QT

If you are intimidated by air fryers, the Instant Pot Vortex is the one to get. It is incredibly simple. You get a single knob and a handful of one-touch presets for fries, chicken, and veggies. You just push the button, set the time, and walk away. It does exactly what it says.

The Vortex is also one of the quietest models in published testing. It won't drown out your TV, which is a big plus. The 4-quart size is perfect for a small household, and the basket is genuinely dishwasher safe. Owner reports describe it holding up through several cycles without warping.

The main downside is that the presets are sometimes a little conservative. You might need to add a couple of minutes to get your food as crispy as you like. Also, the window on the front is not very useful because it's quite dark and hard to see through.

**Who should buy it:** This is for the absolute beginner who wants a no-fuss appliance from a brand they already trust. It is the most foolproof option on this list.

## Ninja AF080

The Ninja AF080 is a bit of a wildcard. It comes with two separate 2-quart baskets, not one big one. This is a game-changer if you often cook two things at once, like chicken nuggets for the kids and roasted broccoli for you. You can set different times and temperatures for each side, and a "Smart Finish" feature ensures they both finish at the same time.

The "Max Crisp" function is no joke. It uses a higher fan speed to get food significantly crunchier than standard air frying. Owner reviews describe it doing a phenomenal job on frozen mozzarella sticks and empanadas. This is the second-best performer in terms of raw cooking power.

The downside is the capacity. Two 2-quart baskets might sound like a lot, but each one is really only suitable for a single serving. If you are cooking for more than two people, this is not the right choice. It also takes up a bit more counter space than a standard single-basket model.

**Who should buy it:** This is for couples or individuals who want to cook a full meal at once without using multiple pans.

## Gourmia 6-Quart

The Gourmia 6-Quart is the value king. For around $35, you are getting a massive 6-quart capacity that can handle a whole 5-pound chicken or a huge batch of fries for the entire family. The 10 digital presets cover all the basics, and the LED display is bright and easy to read.

The performance is surprisingly solid. It hits the right temperatures and produces crispy results, though it is slightly less even than the Cosori. You might need to shake the basket a bit more frequently to avoid hot spots. The build quality is mostly plastic, but it doesn't feel dangerously cheap.

The biggest downside is the design. It's tall and bulky, so it needs a bit of vertical clearance under your cabinets. The basket is also quite heavy when full. But for the price and capacity, these are minor complaints.

**Who should buy it:** This is for larger families on a strict budget who need to cook big portions quickly.

## Chefman 3.7 Quart

The Chefman is the most approachable air fryer on this list. It uses two simple analog dials: one for temperature and one for a 30-minute timer. There are no confusing menus or digital displays to fiddle with. You just set it and let it cook.

It has a few things the others don't. First, there is a genuine see-through window on the front, so you can watch your food crisp up without pulling the basket out. Second, the basket is non-stick and truly easy to clean. We also appreciated the cool-touch handle, which doesn't burn your fingers when you open it.

Performance-wise, it's adequate but not stellar. The 1000-watt heater is on the lower end, so cook times are slightly longer, and getting that deep golden-brown crisp requires a few extra minutes. It also doesn't get as hot as some others, maxing out at 400°F.

**Who should buy it:** This is perfect for seniors, students, or anyone who just wants a simple, reliable appliance without any techy features.

## Philips TurboStar

The Philips TurboStar is the smallest on this list, but it uses the same patented Rapid Air technology found in their expensive full-size models. The key difference is the unique star-shaped bottom plate that forces hot air to circulate in a vortex. This directly impacts the food, creating a crunch that rivals deep frying.

Reviewers report that homemade potato wedges came out the crispiest of all seven models. The fat is also collected in a separate tray at the bottom, which makes it very easy to drain and clean. The included cookbook is also described as genuinely helpful.

The downside is the size. At only 1.8 quarts, it is really only good for a single person or as a second air fryer for sides. It is also the loudest model among those covered, with a high-pitched fan whir. It is a trade-off for the performance.

**Who should buy it:** This is for the single cook or minimalist who prioritizes crispy texture above all else and doesn't need to cook for a crowd.

## Bella Pro Series 4.5QT

Don't let the low price fool you; the Bella Pro Series is a solid performer. It is available at many discount retailers and is often on sale for under $30. For that price, you get a generous 4.5-quart basket, a simple digital display, and eight presets that are actually accurate.

The build quality is better than expected. The basket is heavy and has a sturdy locking mechanism. It's not the most powerful, but it cooks evenly and gets the job done for everyday meals like frozen fries, chicken tenders, and reheating leftovers. The controls are straightforward, with a touchscreen that responds well.

The main drawback is the lack of a "max crisp" or "air crisp" mode. It is a standard air fryer, so it won't give you that extra-shattering crunch. It also runs a little hot, so you may need to check your food a few minutes earlier than you think.

**Who should buy it:** This is for the bargain hunter who wants a reliable, no-frills air fryer that won't break the bank.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many watts should a budget air fryer have?

Aim for at least 1400 watts. Models below that figure tend to need longer cook times and produce less crispy results, particularly with frozen foods. The Cosori Pro LE, for example, uses a 1500-watt element.

### Which size air fryer fits a household of one or two people?

Three to four quarts is described as the sweet spot for one or two people, since it holds a small chicken or a reasonable batch of fries without dominating the counter. The Instant Pot Vortex and Cosori Pro LE both fall in that range at 4 quarts, while the Philips TurboStar is far smaller at 1.8 quarts and suits a single cook.

### Are the baskets on these air fryers dishwasher safe?

It varies by model. The Instant Pot Vortex basket is described as genuinely dishwasher safe, with owner reports of it holding up through several cycles without warping, but some baskets that claim dishwasher safety warp easily. Hand washing is recommended for the Cosori Pro LE basket to extend the life of its non-stick coating.
